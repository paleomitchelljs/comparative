Scope and status. Every anatomical statement here is a paraphrase of a cited source, not a verbatim quotation, and every homology claim is a hypothesis attributed to a named author. Confidence labels reflect the strength of support reported in the source literature, not certainty.

Reading the taxon tables. A blank or absent row means the muscle was not found in the exemplar species examined by that source. It does not mean the muscle is absent from the whole clade — Abdala & Diogo (2010) document several muscles present in one lizard and absent in another. Inferred marks fossil reconstructions, which are bracket-and-correlate inferences rather than observations.

Attachments. The Skeleton view is bone-first: pick an element, see what attaches to it, optionally as recorded in one taxon. Where a taxon has no attachments on record, the consensus is shown and marked inherited — that means nobody has recorded it, not that it is known to match. Attachment shifts on a muscle page are computed by diffing recorded attachments against the earliest taxon with data, so they are derived from the dataset rather than asserted.

Serial correspondences between forelimb and hindlimb muscles follow Diogo & Molnar (2014) and denote topological equivalence. They are not claims of serial homology in the strict ancestral-duplication sense, which that paper rejects.

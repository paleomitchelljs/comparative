# Higashiyama et al. (2016) — Vagal cardiac nerves and the head–trunk interface

## Citation

Higashiyama H, Hirasawa T, Oisi Y, Sugahara F, Hyodo S, Kanai Y, Kuratani S. 2016.
On the vagal cardiac nerves, with special reference to the early evolution of the
head–trunk interface. *Journal of Morphology* 277: 1146–1158.

## Question

The vagus innervates the heart as well as the posterior visceral arches. In amniotes
the anterior and posterior cardiac branches arise from the branchial and intestinal
portions of the vagus and innervate the arterial and venous poles of the heart
respectively. How did that pattern evolve? The obstacle has been a lack of
morphological data on the vagus in basal vertebrates.

## What it gives

Observations of the vagus in basal vertebrates, set against the **circumpharyngeal
ridge** — the arc-shaped boundary marking the caudal limit of the postotic cranial
neural crest, which is where the head–trunk interface is visible in an embryo.

It sits inside a cluster of results that together define that interface: loss of
the posterior gills, caudal shift of the heart, and the acquisition of neck
musculature.

## What it is used for here

Cited on **`branchial-constrictors`** as background. The dataset's cranial and
pectoral records turn on where branchiomeric territory ends — that is what makes
`protractor-pectoralis` the defining muscle of the head–trunk boundary and why its
innervation being vagal rather than spinal is load-bearing.

**No occurrence rows.** This is developmental and neuroanatomical evidence about a
boundary, not a description of a muscle's attachments.

## Relevance to comparative anatomy teaching

The nerve half of the head–trunk boundary argument, and a good corrective to
treating the boundary as a line on a skeleton. Pair with **Sefton et al. (2016)** on
the cucullaris and **Naumann et al. (2017)** on where it develops from.

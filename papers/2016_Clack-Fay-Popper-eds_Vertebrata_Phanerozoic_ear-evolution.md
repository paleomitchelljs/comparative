# Clack, Fay & Popper, eds (2016) — Evolution of the Vertebrate Ear

## Citation

Clack JA, Fay RR, Popper AN (eds). 2016. *Evolution of the Vertebrate Ear: Evidence
from the Fossil Record*. Springer Handbook of Auditory Research 59.

## What it is, and what it is used for here

An edited volume, cited on one record — **`stapedius`** — as background rather than
as a source of rows.

The reason it is cited at all: this dataset treats the shark's hyomandibula, the
lizard's columella and the mammal's stapes as **one skeletal element** under
per-taxon names. That is the rule that keeps the shark's depressor hyomandibulae and
the mammal's stapedius visibly on the same bone, and it needs the
hyomandibula-to-stapes transformation to be well enough established to hang an
element on. This volume is where that fossil evidence is assembled.

## Not mined

**No occurrence rows come from it**, and none should without reading the individual
chapters. An edited volume is not a single argument, and citing it as one would be
the same error as citing a review for an observation.

If the `stapedius` record or the middle-ear elements are ever revisited, the
chapters rather than the volume are what to cite.

## Relevance to comparative anatomy teaching

The reference for the single most-taught transformation in vertebrate comparative
anatomy, and the place to send a student who wants the fossil evidence rather than
the textbook summary of it. Pair with **Gai et al. (2022)** on the spiracular region,
which is the other half of the same story.

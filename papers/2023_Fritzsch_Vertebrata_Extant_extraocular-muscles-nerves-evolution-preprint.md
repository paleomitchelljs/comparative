# Fritzsch 2023 — evolution + development of extraocular nerves and muscles in vertebrates (preprint)

**Citation.** Fritzsch, B. 2023. Evolution and development of extra-ocular nerves and muscles in vertebrates. Preprints.org, DOI: 10.20944/preprints202306.0416.v1. Posted 6 June 2023. **NOT peer-reviewed.**

**Publication status caveat.** MDPI *Preprints* posting. Single author (Bernd Fritzsch, University of Iowa — Department of Biology + Department of Otolaryngology). Has not been through journal peer review; no indication of subsequent publication as of the filing date. Use as a secondary literature pointer, cite primary sources directly (most come from Fritzsch's own earlier papers + collaborators, well-referenced).

## Question

What is the distribution of the six classical extraocular muscles (EOMs) + retractor bulbi + levator palpebrae + accessory muscles across vertebrates, and how do the three ocular motor nerves (CNIII oculomotor, CNIV trochlear, CNVI abducens) map onto them developmentally and evolutionarily?

> "Extraocular eye muscles (EOMs) are innervated by axons of three ocular motor nuclei, the oculomotor (CNIII), trochlear (CNIV), and abducens (CNVI) neurons. The purpose of this review is to analyze the origin of ocular motor neurons, define the pattern of innervation of nerve fibers that project to the EOMs, provide an overview of vestibular pathway inputs to ocular motor nuclei, and describe congenital disorders that alter the development of ocular motor neurons."

## Scope

- Taxa: Myxiniformes, Petromyzontiformes, Chondrichthyes (elasmobranchs, holocephalans), Lungfish, Teleostei, *Polypterus*, *Latimeria*, Caecilians, Salamanders, Frogs + Toads, Sauropsids, Mammals.
- Clade in compilation: Vertebrata.
- Time: Extant (comparative review).
- Time depth: ~520 Mya origin of EOMs to present.

## Methods

Literature review. Fritzsch-style synthesis using primary data from:
- Dye tracing (HRP, DiI) of cranial nerves (Fritzsch, own lab + collaborators).
- 3H-thymidine + BrdU + EdU proliferation labelling for motor-neuron birthdating (rat, chicken, zebrafish, mouse).
- In situ hybridization + knockout mouse literature for developmental genes: *Myf5*, *Pitx2*, *Robo*, *Slit*, *Netrin*, *Unc5*, *Nrp1/2*, *Sema3a/f*, *Plxna1*, *Chn1* (α2-chimaerin), *Kif21a*, *Epha4*, *Efna5*, *Hoxa1*, *Mafb*, *Pcdh17*, *Shh*, *Ptch*, *Smo*, *Gli1-3*, *En1/2*, *Foxa1/2*, *Lmx1b*, *Nurr1*, *Pitx3*, *Fgf8*, *Wnt1*, *Otx2/Gbx2*, *Sall4*.
- Mutant mouse data for congenital cranial dysinnervation disorders (CCDDs): ptosis, strabismus, misrouting.
- Comparative MRI + histology data from primary literature (cited throughout).

## Findings (by section)

1. **Six classical EOMs and their innervation (Table 1).**
   - **CNIII (oculomotor):** superior rectus (SR, contralateral dorsal), inferior rectus (IR, ipsilateral ventral), medial rectus (MR, ipsilateral ventral — tetrapods only, absent in lampreys + chondrichthyans), inferior oblique (IO, ipsilateral ventral), levator palpebrae superioris (LPS, mammals only, contralateral dorsal), nasal rectus NR (chondrichthyans + lungfish only, contralateral dorsal).
   - **CNIV (trochlear):** superior oblique SO (gnathostome tetrapods), caudal oblique CO (lampreys). Always contralateral.
   - **CNVI (abducens):** lateral rectus LR = ventral rectus VR in lampreys = posterior rectus PR. Retractor bulbi RB in tetrapods (may be absent in microchiropterans + primates). Caudal rectus CR in lampreys (second EOM, lost in chondrichthyans + lungfish + actinopterygians). Basicranial rectus BR in *Latimeria* — a huge abducens-innervated muscle **not connected to the eye**, inserts into a tendon that moves the intracranial joint. Basicranial muscles likely plesiomorphic for sarcopterygians (including *Eusthenopteron*), converted to retractor bulbi in most tetrapods.
2. **Rhombomere origin of motor nuclei.** CNIII + CNIV develop from r0 / midbrain (CNIII strictly midbrain; CNIV at the midbrain–hindbrain junction). CNVI develops from r4–r6 with variable contribution across clades: lamprey r4–6; chondrichthyans r6; lungfish r5; teleost + sauropsid r5–6; chicken r5–6; lungfish + amphibians + mammals r5. Hagfish lack all ocular motor neurons — the only vertebrate group with **no extraocular muscles**.
3. **Headline evolutionary table (Fig. 3 cladogram, ~520 Mya).** Hagfish: 0 EOMs. Lampreys: 3 CNIII + 1 CNIV + 2 CNVI muscles, r4–6 abducens. Gnathostomes: 4 CNIII + 1 CNIV + 1 CNVI + retractor bulbi. Chondrichthyans + lungfish retain a **contralateral nasal rectus (NR)** innervated by a dorsal ramus of CNIII, absent from crown osteichthyans. Tetrapods + *Latimeria*: medial rectus (MR) added, innervated by a ventral branch of CNIII (ipsilateral). Caecilians have a **tentacular retractor muscle** homologous to the retractor bulbi. Salamanders + frogs + toads have standard retractor bulbi. Sauropsids: retractor bulbi + quadratus + pyramidalis (the two nictitating-membrane muscles). Mammals: retractor bulbi (often reduced or absent in microchiroptera + primates) + levator palpebrae superioris (unique mammalian CNIII muscle).
4. **Origin of CNIII contralateral projection.** In all vertebrates a contralateral CNIII population supplies the superior rectus. Lampreys have three branches (one contra + two ipsi). Chondrichthyans + lungfish have one contra + one contra NR + two ipsi. Bony fish, *Latimeria*, tetrapods have one contra + three ipsi (MR + IR + IO). Mammals add bilateral innervation to LPS.
5. **CNIV dorsal-exit is unique to gnathostomes.** Trochlear axons exit the brainstem **dorsally** then cross midline to innervate the contralateral SO. In lampreys CNIV develops next to the cerebellum and exits bilaterally; in gnathostomes it develops next to the ocular motor neurons + forms long dorsally-crossing fibres. Cutting the nerve in frogs + chicken produces a redirection where axons interact with adjacent CNIII fibres to innervate SO. CNIV migrates late from midbrain/hindbrain into a more rostral position.
6. **CNVI provides more than lateral rectus.** In *Latimeria* CNVI supplies the basicranial rectus (huge, not connected to eye). In lampreys CNVI supplies LR + caudal rectus (CR). In tetrapods CNVI supplies LR + retractor bulbi. Retractor bulbi is equivalent to the CR of lampreys and the basicranial rectus of sarcopterygians; it converts to **quadratus + pyramidalis** (nictitating-membrane muscles) in sauropsids, and is reduced or absent in microchiropterans + primates + some other mammals.
7. **EOMs derive from paraxial mesoderm head cavities.** Three pairs of head cavities = epithelial mesodermal coeloms form in register with pharyngeal pouches, generating three cranial "somites" (metameric). *Myf5* is the first gene expressed in these mesoderm regions; migrating myoblasts form the four CNIII muscles, the dorsal CNIV SO, and the CNVI LR + retractor bulbi precursors near the trigeminal ganglion. *Myf5* null mutants have no EOMs. *Pitx2* is required to prevent EOM degeneration after initial formation. EOMs are unusual: unaffected by Duchenne muscular dystrophy but affected by myasthenia gravis; differ from branchial + trunk muscles in fibre composition + innervation pattern.
8. **Developmental axon guidance.** CNIII extension begins ~E10 in mouse, reaches EOMs by ~E11, segregates to all six muscles by E13. Guidance genes:
   - *Semaphorin 3a/f* via *Neuropilin 1/2* + *Plexin a1* + *α2-chimaerin (Chn1)* — repulsion/defasciculation.
   - *Netrin 1* + *Unc5c* + *Dcc* — bifunctional attraction/repulsion for CNIV decussation.
   - *Slit 1/2* + *Robo 1/2* — midline crossing control; deletion disrupts contralateral SR CNIII.
   - *Kif21a* mutants — CNIII axons prematurely exit dorsal root, fail to reach SR + LPS → reduced upward gaze + ptosis (CCDD phenotype).
   - *Epha4 + Efna5* + *Pcdh17* + *Mafb* + *Sall4* — CNVI guidance.
9. **Vestibular inputs to ocular motor nuclei.** Teleosts, frogs, birds, mammals have a specific stereotyped pattern. Chondrichthyans show a different combined ipsilateral + contralateral pattern. Lamprey data partial (no horizontal semicircular canal — horizontal VOR not comparable). Not the same wiring diagram across clades even though the EOMs themselves are nearly identical.
10. **Congenital cranial dysinnervation disorders (CCDDs).** Paralytic strabismus, ptosis, synkinesis (Duane retraction syndrome, Möbius sequence) all map onto mouse mutants in the genes above. CCDDs provide a translational handle on the developmental framework.

## Limitations

- **Not peer-reviewed.** Single-author preprint. Figures derive from author's own earlier papers (cited heavily). Acknowledge provenance when teaching.
- Density of genes, clades, and developmental stages makes the text hard to follow as a primary teaching source — better used as an index into primary literature.
- Vestibular input section is sparse (brief treatment) despite being promised as a structural pillar of the abstract.
- *Latimeria* basicranial muscle organisation flagged as requiring modern antibody/tubulin labelling (existing data old); the hypothesis that basicranial muscle is the ancestral sarcopterygian condition + progenitor of tetrapod retractor bulbi remains inference.
- Table 1 classifies "caudal rectus" as lamprey-specific and "NR" as chondrichthyan + lungfish-specific, but the homology across these + teleost "additional muscle" branches is not critically evaluated.
- Congenital cranial dysinnervation disorder (CCDD) coverage depends entirely on mouse-model data; translation to clinical phenotypes flagged but not systematically reviewed.
- Reference style is dense + sometimes cryptic (numbered, e.g., [9,16,53]) without clear primary-citation separation from reviews.

**Pedagogic headline.** The six "classical" extraocular muscles of tetrapods are not a stable, conserved set across vertebrates. Hagfish have zero; lampreys have six but with a different complement (caudal rectus, no MR); chondrichthyans + lungfish have a nasal rectus that the rest of gnathostomes lack; mammals add a levator palpebrae superioris; caecilians repurpose retractor bulbi as a tentacular retractor; *Latimeria* has a huge abducens-innervated basicranial muscle that doesn't move the eye at all. The homology is at the motor-nucleus + rhombomere level (r0 CNIII, midbrain-hindbrain junction CNIV, r4–6 CNVI) + the gene regulatory logic (*Myf5* → *Pitx2*), not at the named-muscle level.

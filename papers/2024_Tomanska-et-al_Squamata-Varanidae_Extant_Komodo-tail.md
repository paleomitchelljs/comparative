# Tomańska et al. (2024) — The Komodo dragon's tail

## Citation

Tomańska A, Stawinoga M, Szturo K, Styczyńska M, Klećkowska-Nawrot J, Janeczek M,
Goździewska-Harłajczuk K, Melnyk O, Gębarowski T. 2024. Biological significance of
the Komodo dragon's tail (*Varanus komodoensis*, Varanidae). *Animals* 14: 2142.

## What it covers

The tail as a whole organ: skeleton, musculature, accumulated fat tissue, spinal
cord and anal glands. Framed around conservation, since the species is threatened
and material is scarce.

One of three papers from overlapping author groups covering the Komodo dragon
region by region — **Kępa et al. (2023)** on the thoracic limb, **Tomańska et al.
(2025)** on the hindlimb, and this one on the tail.

## Scored

1 occurrence row on *Varanus komodoensis* — `caudal-musculature`.

The row records the tail as a substantial locomotor and defensive organ, with
caudal muscle mass contributing counterbalance during bipedal rearing.

## Limitations

**Three origin/insertion mentions in the whole paper.** This is a functional and
morphological account rather than an attachment description, and it is not a row
source. Scoring more from it would mean inventing attachment sites it does not
state.

Its value here is that the caudal musculature record has very few animals on it at
all, and a large varanid is a useful one to have.

## Relevance to comparative anatomy teaching

Good for the point that the axial system does not stop at the pelvis, and that a
tail can be a locomotor organ rather than an appendage. Pair with **Ercoli et al.
(2012)** for mustelid lumbar and tail myology and with **Schilling (2011)** for the
axial system across craniates.

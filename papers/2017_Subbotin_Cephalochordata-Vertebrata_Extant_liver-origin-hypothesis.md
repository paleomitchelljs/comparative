# Subbotin 2017 — Arguments on the origin of the vertebrate liver and the Amphioxus hepatic diverticulum: A hypothesis on evolutionary novelties

## Citation

Subbotin VM. 2017. Arguments on the origin of the vertebrate liver and the Amphioxus hepatic diverticulum: A hypothesis on evolutionary novelties. *Pis'ma v Vavilovskii Zhurnal* / Letters to Vavilov Journal (hypothesis article). Available at http://www.bionet.nsc.ru/vogis/download/hypothesis/appx.1pdf. ResearchGate preprint.

## Question

What is the phylogenetic precursor of the vertebrate liver / portal-vein system, a complex organ with unique vascularization that is already fully formed in Cyclostomata? Experts agree the *Amphioxus* (Cephalochordata) **midgut (hepatic) diverticulum** is the most plausible homologous precursor — but the Homology Principle obliges a next question: what could be the precursor of the already-elaborate *Amphioxus* diverticulum? Subbotin's hypothesis: the diverticulum evolved from the **yolk sac of a preceding advanced chordate ancestor**, with the yolk sac ceasing to function in food storage, becoming internalized, and acquiring digestive-organ functions.

## Taxa and material

- *Amphioxus* (*Branchiostoma floridae*) — anatomical reference (Fig. 1: H&E-stained paraffin section at the pharynx showing the hepatic diverticulum).
- Comparative developmental data from: **lamprey larva** (Baxter 1957; Parker 1878), **Russian sturgeon** *Acipenser* (Schmalhausen 1991), **teleost fishes** (yolk sac vascularization review, Mossman 1948; Isogai & Horiguchi 1997).
- Historical sources: Kovalevsky 1865 (original Russian magisterial thesis on *Amphioxus lanceolatus* development), Kovalevsky 1867 (German), Kovalevsky 1951 (Russian reprint). Darwin 1859 / 1861 / 1872 quotes from *Origin of Species*.
- **No new primary data.** This is a theoretical/literature-synthesis hypothesis paper.

## Time frame

Extant plus evolutionary inference back to **Early–Middle Cambrian** (Benton 2005; Mallatt & Holland 2013; Morris & Whittington 1979) through **Ordovician** (Chaline 1990; Dineley 1964). Cephalochordate fossil evidence is sparse; the paper reconstructs from extant morphology + developmental data.

## Methods

- **Inference to the Best Explanation** (Harman 1965; Lipton 2003; Thagard 1978) — Darwinian comparative reasoning. No experimental data. Paper is explicitly a theoretical/hypothesis contribution.
- **Homology principle** (Shubin et al. 2009) applied to the organ: portal-vein + liver arrangement appears fully formed in cyclostomes, so a homologous precursor must exist in the invertebrate chordate lineage.
- **Literature synthesis** of historical (Kovalevsky 1865; Shore 1891; Weichert 1953), mid-20th-century (Romer 1959, 1967, 1972; Mossman 1948; Baxter 1957; Schmalhausen 1991), and recent (Bhattacharya et al. 2008; Han et al. 2006; Wang et al. 2015) evidence.

## Findings

### Part 1 — Amphioxus diverticulum as homologous precursor of vertebrate liver

1. **Elaborate vascularization of the Amphioxus hepatic diverticulum.** Fig. 2 circulation scheme: visceral post-capillary venous blood from the caudal intestine is collected into an **unpaired subintestine vein**, which brings blood to the diverticulum; the diverticulum's capillaries again collect into a single vein — the **vena Cardinales posterior** (analog of vena cava in vertebrates). This is the homologous precursor to the vertebrate portal vein / liver system.
2. **Shore 1891 and Weichert 1953 first identified the portal-system analogy.** Shore: "we must not lose sight of the fact that this animal (*Amphioxus* – VMS) has now been shown to possess a 'portal' system of veins quite comparable to that of higher vertebrates, and also... no invertebrate has anything like a 'portal' system of blood-vessels been shown to exist."
3. **Vertebrate liver-specific proteins are expressed in the Amphioxus diverticulum**: glutathione-S-transferase, plasminogen-like protein, antithrombin, cytochrome P450 (Bhattacharya et al. 2008; Fan et al. 2007b; Liang & Zhang 2006; Liang et al. 2006; Mizuta & Kubokawa 2007). "This expression supports the homology hypothesis."
4. **Vitellogenin production.** "The Amphioxus diverticulum is the sole tissue producing vitellogenin in Amphioxus (Han et al., 2006; Wang et al., 2015)." In all vertebrates vitellogenin is synthesized by the liver (never by oocytes themselves). "Reinforces the homology of digestive diverticulum to vertebrate liver."
5. **Co-evolution of insulin-producing tissue + liver.** Fig. 3 (Heller 2010): in invertebrates, insulin + glucagon peptides are produced by neural cells; in protochordates (amphioxus, tunicates), scattered epithelial cells of the hepatic diverticulum + caudal intestine express insulin-like growth factor but are **not yet organized into islet organs**. Cyclostomes are the first chordates with compact islet organs (Brockman bodies) + liver **simultaneously**. The transition of pancreatic-family hormone expression from neural to intestinal-epithelial cells + portal circulation back to the diverticulum together promoted the diverticulum → liver differentiation.
6. **Bottom line of Part 1**: "The hypothesis that *Cephalochordate*'s hepatic diverticulum is a homologous phylogenetic precursor of vertebrate liver sounds very substantial and is shared by most experts."

### Part 2 — Hypothesis: Amphioxus diverticulum evolved from a yolk sac

7. **Driving question (per the Homology Principle): what is the precursor of the Amphioxus diverticulum itself?** "No relevant evidence from putatively preceding forms (existing or fossil)."
8. **Proposed homologous precursor = yolk sac** of a preceding advanced chordate. Stretched + protruded from the ventral site of the Lancelet body, with its skin and vasculature intact, a midgut diverticulum would be "morphologically homologous to a yolk sac" (Fig. 4, schematic).
9. **Internal consistency check.** Yolk sac contains endodermal + mesenchymal + ectodermal tissues — all present in bilaterally symmetrical animals. No structural violation.
10. **Shore 1891 already suggested embryonic nutrition origin**: "that this organ primarily arose as an embryonic 'organ of nutrition,' evolved *pari passu* with the evolution of those vertebrates which produced large yolked ova."
11. **Objection**: Amphioxus has microlecithal/alecithal oocytes (traditionally considered "primary alecithal") and no yolk sac. Subbotin counters: ovum size can reverse across clades depending on nutrient availability (lecithotrophy ↔ planktotrophy; Allen & Pernet 2007; Allen et al. 2006; Pernet & McHugh 2010). Amphioxus could have descended from an ancestor with a yolk sac, with the yolk sac secondarily lost from oocyte mode + diverged for digestive function.
12. **Lamprey larva developmental evidence** (Baxter 1957): "In these larvae the blood can be seen traversing the lateral walls of the intestine at the anterior end of the yolk mass and by this route a steady trickle of blood reaches the now mid-ventral sub-intestinal vein. In this vessel the blood passes forwards to the liver, which has now reached the stage of a hollow sac, and from anastomosing vessels in its walls the hepatic blood is returned to the heart." Lamprey larva has **both** a yolk sac + a developing liver, with similar vascular pattern. "The fact that the lamprey has two hollow sacs (liver and yolk sac) with similar vascularization patterns is puzzling" — Subbotin speculates a **gene-duplication origin** where one copy took on the new digestive/secretory function and the other retained food storage.
13. **Sturgeon** (Schmalhausen 1991): at hatching the digestive system has an alimentary canal + digestive glands + liver + dorsal pancreas; the alimentary canal divides into a widened anterior yolk-sac part and a narrow posterior part — same anatomical relation between yolk sac + intestine as Amphioxus between hepatic diverticulum + caudal intestine.
14. **Teleost yolk sac blood supply** (Mossman 1948): "The blood supply of the yolk sac of teleost fishes comes from somatic veins, such as the caudal and cardinals, instead of from vitelline arteries branching off from the aorta as in amniotes." Isogai & Horiguchi 1997 later confirmed the early participation of subintestinal + posterior cardinal veins. **Favors homology between yolk sac and Amphioxus diverticulum.**
15. **Romer's chordate-evolution framework** (Romer 1959, 1967, 1972; Romer & Parsons 1986a). Chordate phylogenesis began with primitive sessile ("attached"), "visceral", "arm-feeding" animals → evolved into sessile PP filter-feeding ancestors → free-swimming larva → motile advanced chordate = ancestor of basal vertebrate + Amphioxus. Subbotin's hypothesis is congruent with Romer's model.
16. **Objection from molecular phylogenetics**: recent analyses (Delsuc et al. 2006, 2008) place Tunicata as sister taxon to Vertebrata, not Cephalochordata. Subbotin flags this but notes Ivanova-Kazas 1995's morphological analysis still favored Cephalochordata as the sister of Vertebrata; the argument remains depending on which signal is weighted. The yolk-sac hypothesis is compatible with either topology, but the specific "motile advanced chordate with yolk sac" ancestor model fits Romer's older tree better.
17. **Tunicata diversity of body plans** is flagged as an alternative source of ancestor morphology not explored in detail.

## Limitations

- **This is a hypothesis / opinion article**, not an experimental study. No new data — the argument is structured as "Inference to the Best Explanation" with Darwinian reasoning.
- **No fossil support.** The precursor of the Amphioxus diverticulum would be pre-Cambrian or Early Cambrian; fossil cephalochordate record is too sparse to test.
- **Yolk-sac ancestor requires Amphioxus to have lost yolked ova secondarily.** Author acknowledges that the traditional view treats Amphioxus microlecithal oocytes as **primary alecithal**. Secondary loss is plausible but not demonstrated.
- **Tunicata-as-sister-to-Vertebrata molecular phylogenies** (Delsuc et al. 2006, 2008) conflict with Romer's morphological tree that the hypothesis relies on. Author acknowledges this openly.
- **Lamprey larva two-sac parallel** is cited as support, but lamprey liver and yolk sac are not directly shown to be serial homologues — the gene-duplication speculation is unsupported.
- **Only self-citation to a 1999 Inverness conference presentation + a 2015 SMBE poster.** No primary data collected.
- **Publication venue.** Russian-language journal (*Pis'ma v Vavilovskii Zhurnal*), published in English via ResearchGate with the Russian abstract. Limited peer-review track record.
- **Author is V.M. Subbotin, Arrowhead Pharmaceuticals + University of Pittsburgh School of Medicine**, not a classical comparative-anatomy/cephalochordate-development specialist. Writing draws heavily on historical authors (Kovalevsky, Shore, Weichert) and Romer.
- **No molecular-developmental test.** The hypothesis predicts that genes specifying yolk-sac endoderm in vertebrates (GATA, Sox17) should pattern the Amphioxus diverticulum; the paper does not invoke this evidence.

## Relevance to comparative anatomy teaching

- **Darwin + Homology Principle as teaching anchor.** Paper's structure — "for every complex organ ask: what is the homologous precursor?" — is a clean teaching case. Opens with the full Darwin quote from *Origin of Species* Ch. 6 "Difficulties of Theory." Pair with Cook et al. 2017 + Lewis & Hanken 2017 (shared morphogenetic programs, pulmonary induction) as "organs descend with modifications from homologs, look for the developmental logic" teaching theme.
- **Two-step hypothesis building.** Part 1 (diverticulum ↔ vertebrate liver) is the consensus. Part 2 (diverticulum ↔ yolk sac of a preceding chordate) is speculative. Useful for teaching **how much of a clade-wide homology is consensus vs. how much is still contentious** in the comparative-anatomy literature.
- **The liver-portal system is remarkably conserved.** Elias & Bengelsdorf 1952: "liver architecture shares the same fundamental plan in all vertebrates, from low to the highest subclasses. It is a continuous mass of cells tunneled by a labyrinth of lacunae, in which the network of sinusoids is suspended. This structure is termed a muralium ('wallwork')." Teach as the "organ that is already basically finished at the cyclostome level" contrast to the heart (which is **not** already finished — see Cook et al. 2017 table of amniote cardiac differences).
- **Co-evolution of liver + endocrine pancreas.** Fig. 3 reproduces Heller 2010's evolution-of-islet-organ chart. Pair with Diogo 2008 (pharyngeal-arch muscle homology) and Fritzsch 1996 (lancelet vs. craniate CNS) for a "protochordate-to-vertebrate organ-by-organ transition" lecture module. Key data points: islet organ first appears in Cyclostomata simultaneously with the liver.
- **Vitellogenin-synthesis site.** The diverticulum alone produces vitellogenin in Amphioxus; the liver alone produces it in all vertebrates. A clean biochemical-developmental homology argument that doesn't depend on anatomy alone. Teaching use: biochemical vs. topological homology arguments can reinforce each other.
- **Amphioxus diverticulum as the **only** visceral asymmetry in an otherwise bilateral chordate**. "Appearance of the diverticulum in Amphioxus on the right side represents phyletically the earliest and most significant visceral asymmetry that occurs in chordate animals that otherwise maintain bilateral symmetry through adult life." Worth noting for the teaching of **bilateral symmetry + visceral asymmetry** as independent axes of body-plan specification.
- **Limitations as the primary teaching message**: this is an example of how far comparative anatomy + homology reasoning can take you when experimental data are lacking, and also where they leave off. The yolk-sac hypothesis is a "best explanation" with no decisive test — a useful exercise in evaluating a scientific hypothesis without rejecting it outright.
- **Romer's visceral → somatic chordate model.** Rare to see Romer 1959/1967/1972 cited with specificity in recent papers. Useful reading to show the continuity (and the argument) of mid-20th-century vertebrate-evolution synthesis with modern molecular work. Pair with Fritzsch 1996 on cephalochordate vs. craniate CNS — both papers honor Romer-era morphological reasoning while flagging its limits.
- **Caveat for teaching**: this is not a data paper and should be framed that way if assigned. A very good hypothesis, uneven evidence base. Useful as a prompt for discussion: "does the yolk-sac precursor hypothesis survive molecular phylogenetic constraints?" (answer: partly, if Tunicata are excluded from the lineage).

# Godoy et al. (2016) — Postcranial anatomy of *Pissarrachampsa sera*

## Citation

Godoy PL, Bronzati M, Eltink E, Marsola JCA, Cidade GM, Langer MC, Montefeltro FC.
2016. Postcranial anatomy of *Pissarrachampsa sera* (Crocodyliformes,
Baurusuchidae) from the Late Cretaceous of Brazil: insights on lifestyle and
phylogenetic significance. *PeerJ* 4: e2075.

## Why it was reached for

The postcranium of Crocodyliformes has been neglected — most descriptions are
skulls — even though the postcranium is where the group's lifestyle diversity
lives, from terrestrial through semi-aquatic to fully marine.

For this dataset the value is narrower and more specific: **it labels muscle scars
directly on the described elements.** That is an osteological-correlate source on a
crocodyliform, which is the grade between the sphenosuchians and the crown, and
there is very little else at that node.

## Taxa and material

*Pissarrachampsa sera*, Adamantina Formation, Bauru Group, Late Cretaceous of
Brazil. Preserved: dorsal vertebrae, partial forelimb, pelvic girdle and hindlimbs.
Compared against other baurusuchids and crocodyliforms, with body-size and mass
estimates.

## Scored

7 occurrence rows on *Pissarrachampsa sera*, all `inferred` — this is a fossil and
every row is a reconstruction from a scar rather than an observation of a muscle.

The correlates the paper names and the rows carry:

- **Biceps and humeroradialis insertion tubercles** on the radius.
- **Triceps scars** and a **pronator quadratus origin fossa** on the ulna.
- **Iliotibialis rugosities** on the supraacetabular crest.
- A caudofemoralis correlate on the femur.

Each row carries one attachment, which is the honest resolution: a scar locates one
end of a muscle and says nothing about the other.

## Limitations

**A correlate locates an attachment; it does not size the muscle, and its absence
is not evidence of absence.** Pereyra et al. (2019) show histologically that the
same muscle on the same bone leaves Sharpey's fibres in some turtle species and
none in others. Read a missing scar here as missing evidence.

**One specimen, and a partial one.** The forelimb is partial and nothing cranial is
scored from this source.

## Relevance to comparative anatomy teaching

A compact example of the whole osteological-correlate method on a single fossil:
here is the bone, here is the roughened patch, here is the muscle it is inferred to
have carried, and here is the extant animal the inference comes from. Pair with
**Bodenham et al. (2026)** for a stem crocodylomorph and **Burch (2014)** for the
same logic on the bird line, where the bracket is quantified.

# Tomańska et al. (2025) — The Komodo dragon's hindlimb

## Citation

Tomańska A, Stawinoga M, Gębarowski T, Janeczek M, Klećkowska-Nawrot J,
Goździewska-Harłajczuk K, Dobrzyński M. 2025. The musculoskeletal anatomy of the
Komodo dragon's hindlimb (*Varanus komodoensis*, Varanidae). *Animals* 15: 35.

## What it covers

The pelvic limb, by **dissection of all regions**, with radiographic imaging for
overall skeletal structure and **histology for muscle tissue and fibre
architecture**. The muscles are reported as densely arranged, giving a compact and
firm structure.

Companion to **Kępa et al. (2023)** on the thoracic limb and **Tomańska et al.
(2024)** on the tail.

## Scored

2 occurrence rows on *Varanus komodoensis* — `iliofibularis` and `ambiens`.

The iliofibularis row records it stabilising the pelvic region and femur during
movement and allowing hip abduction; Dick & Clemente (2016) include it among the
major varanid locomotor muscles, which is the cross-check.

## Limitations

**Six origin/insertion mentions across the paper.** The descriptions are
functionally framed rather than tabulated, so attachment extraction is partial by
necessity — the paper says what a muscle does more often than where it ends.

That makes it a good source for function and a weak one for rows. It is scored
where a statement is unambiguous and left alone otherwise.

## Relevance to comparative anatomy teaching

The Komodo dragon is the largest living lizard and a natural teaching example for
the sprawling posture at large body size. Pair with **Dick & Clemente (2016)**,
whose scaling series runs from a 7.6 g *Varanus brevicauda* to this animal at 40 kg,
and where the architecture — not the attachments — is what changes.

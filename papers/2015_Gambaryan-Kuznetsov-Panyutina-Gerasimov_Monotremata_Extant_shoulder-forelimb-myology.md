# Gambaryan, Kuznetsov, Panyutina & Gerasimov 2015 — Monotreme shoulder and forelimb myology

## Citation

Gambaryan PP, Kuznetsov AN, Panyutina AA, Gerasimov SV. 2015. Shoulder girdle and forelimb myology of extant Monotremata. *Russian Journal of Theriology* 14(1): 1–56.

## Question

What is the comparative myology of the shoulder girdle and forelimb across **all three extant monotreme genera** — *Zaglossus*, *Tachyglossus*, *Ornithorhynchus* — and what does it tell us about (a) the relative morphological primitivity of monotremes vs. therian mammals, (b) the relative primitivity of Tachyglossidae vs. Ornithorhynchidae, and (c) the value of monotremes as a model for premammalian (mammal-like-reptile) locomotorium reconstruction? Direct rebuttal of Howell (1936): "the myology of *Ornithorhynchus* is so specialized that it is misleading to use it in deducing the primitive mammalian arrangement." Authors disagree explicitly.

## Taxa and material

- ***Zaglossus bruijnii*** (long-beaked echidna) — adult male ZMMU MS579A (alcohol), adult ZMMU MS579B (formalin), 1 uncatalogued adult female (alcohol). The primary subject; first comparative myology of *Zaglossus* since Allen 1912.
- ***Tachyglossus aculeatus*** (short-beaked echidna) — 2 adults (1 formalin, 1 alcohol).
- ***Ornithorhynchus anatinus*** (platypus) — 1 specimen retaining teeth (alcohol; juvenile).

X-ray CT scans of intact specimens before dissection to record natural bone articulation. Sample size is small per species but covers all three living genera — first such comparison.

## Time frame

Extant. Inference framework spans through Mammaliformes / "mammal-like reptiles" / cynodonts back to amphibian tetrapod plesiomorphies.

## Methods

Standard descriptive dissection, each muscle described under origin / insertion / remarks; described in genus order *Zaglossus* → *Tachyglossus* → *Ornithorhynchus*; figures consistently labelled A/B/C in that order. X-ray CT for shoulder girdle, antebrachium, and manus before dissection. Therian myological nomenclature applied wherever possible (explicit reversal of Howell 1937's renaming of monotreme muscles).

**Key methodological position on "primitivity":** Authors explicitly distinguish *primitive* (= simple, homonomic, undifferentiated, widespread at the lower level of organization) from *plesiomorphic* (= ancestral state in a phylogeny). They argue tree-independent morphological criteria of primitivity remain necessary because evolution is mosaic — features regress, get re-recruited via paedomorphosis or atavism, and no single tree accommodates all character distributions. So the paper polarizes characters by appeal to amphibian + reptilian outgroup conditions and to muscle complexity (subdivision count, fasciculation, bilamination), not by character optimization on a tree.

## Findings

1. **Monotremes as a whole retain ancestral mammalian forelimb myology.** Authors explicitly reject Howell's (1936/1937) claim that monotremes are too aberrant to model premammalian locomotorium. Verbatim conclusion: "Among living tetrapods, one cannot find a better model for reconstruction of the locomotorium of the mammal-like reptiles than monotremes, except for their peculiar, anuran-like, ball-and-socket elbow joint."
2. **Primitive features shared by monotremes vs. therians:**
   - Presence of m. levator scapulae intermedius and m. serratus ventralis cervicis accessorius.
   - Underdeveloped m. serratus ventralis thoracis vs. cervicis (more cervical, less thoracic — reverse of the therian condition).
   - Coracoid-plate musculature retained: m. sternocoracoideus, m. costocoracoideus, m. subcoracoideus, m. supracoracoideus.
   - Single undivided m. pectoralis (no superficial/profundus split).
   - m. brachialis divided into ulnar + radial heads.
   - m. extensor carpi radialis insertion symmetric about the metacarpal III mid-axis.
   - m. extensor pollicis et indicis still integrated into a single muscle.
   - m. palmaris longus absent (in *Zaglossus*, present only as a minor fibre bundle of the m. cutaneus trunci).
   - m. flexor digitorum superficialis bellies located **within the manus** (not in the antebrachium as in therians).
3. **Advanced features of monotremes vs. therians:**
   - Extremely distal insertion of m. latissimus dorsi (linked to the swollen monotreme rib cage).
   - Presence of a separate m. latissimus costalis.
   - m. teres minor inserts postaxially.
   - m. triceps longus divided into two heads.
   - Distal insertion of m. extensor carpi ulnaris reaching nearly to digit V.
4. **Tachyglossidae vs. Ornithorhynchus — *Tachyglossidae* is more primitive proximally** (above the elbow):
   - Origin of m. levator scapulae extends beyond the atlas.
   - Less pronounced division between m. serratus ventralis cervicis proprius and accessorius.
   - m. tensor fasciae antebrachii unitary with m. latissimus costalis.
   - Inner surface of scapula occupied predominantly by well-developed **m. supraspinatus**, not m. subscapularis (the inverse of the therian condition; non-therian fossa disposition documented in CT, Fig. 1).
5. ***Ornithorhynchus* is more primitive distally** (below the elbow):
   - m. flexor digitorum profundus caput humerale profundum retains the amphibian m. ulnocarpalis configuration, including the ligamentum flexorium commune transversum.
   - Full set of mm. interossei retained, possibly including m. contrahens V.
   - mm. intermetacarpales present (4 in number — 2 between metacarpals II/III and 2 between III/IV); these are absent in echidnas, contra Kajava 1911 and Howell 1936.
6. **Some monotreme muscles retain configurations more primitive than even living amphibians.** The homonomic subdivision of m. serratus ventralis cervicis into main + accessory portions, and the m. flexor digitorum profundus caput humerale profundum tracing back to the amphibian m. ulnocarpalis, are at the deep end of the primitivity spectrum.
7. **Within *Zaglossus*, individual variation concentrates below the elbow** — variable head counts of mm. flexores breves superficiales, mm. lumbricales, mm. interossei, and variable terminal branching of m. flexor digitorum profundus. Driven by variable degree of marginal-finger reduction within this genus.
8. ***Zaglossus bartoni*** (eastern long-beaked echidna), which retains all five claws (Flannery & Groves 1998), is recommended as an even better model than the *Z. bruijnii* studied here, because it lacks the marginal-finger reduction.
9. **Howell (1937) re-identified the big monotreme infraspinous muscle as m. teres minor.** Authors reject this and revert to the original Westling 1889 / McKay 1894 identification: it is m. infraspinatus. Howell's renaming created knock-on errors in surrounding muscle identifications and led him to introduce many new monotreme-specific names; this paper restores therian-equivalent nomenclature wherever possible.
10. **The carpal "preaxial complex bone"** in the proximal carpal row (called scaphocentralolunatum in some therians like colugos and bats, per Panyutina et al. 2015) is identified as fused **radiale + intermedium + centrale** (per Holmgren 1952). Authors retain the more common terminology.
11. **Humerus has a single spherical condyle** for both radius and ulna with an accessory facet on the radial side — no separate trochlea humeri for the ulna. Anuran-like ball-and-socket elbow. Acknowledged as the major monotreme apomorphy that disqualifies them as locomotorium models for that one joint.

## Limitations

- **Sample sizes very small** — 3 *Zaglossus*, 2 *Tachyglossus*, 1 *Ornithorhynchus*. Individual variation within *Zaglossus* (especially in distal-forelimb variants) is documented but not quantified.
- ***Ornithorhynchus* sample is a single juvenile** with retained teeth. Adult variation untested.
- *Zaglossus bartoni* (the recommended ideal model) is not actually dissected here — only *Z. bruijnii* is.
- Authors' "primitivity" framework is **not phylogenetic.** They explicitly justify this on the grounds that mosaic evolution and atavism make tree-independent criteria necessary, but the result is that their character polarizations are not auditable against a cladistic dataset. A reader who wants to use these characters in a future formal phylogeny will need to re-polarize.
- Russian-language reviewer literature on monotreme anatomy is heavily cited; English-only readers will not be able to verify several historical claims about Westling, McKay, and the Howell controversy.
- The paper rejects Howell 1937's nomenclatural innovations forcefully but does not reconcile its naming with Diogo & Abdala 2010 (the most recent comparative monotreme dissection in English), which had reached different conclusions on a number of muscles. Cross-comparison is left to the reader.

## Relevance to comparative anatomy teaching

A heavyweight reference monograph that should sit on the shelf for the mammalian forelimb lecture. Use it for:

- **The classic teaching argument that monotremes are an excellent model for premammalian forelimb anatomy.** This paper is the most thorough modern (post-Diogo & Abdala 2010) defense of that position. Pair with Diogo & Abdala 2010 for a balanced view — those authors are more cautious about how much *Ornithorhynchus* can stand in for the ancestral state.
- **The "Tachyglossidae primitive proximally / *Ornithorhynchus* primitive distally" partitioning** as a teaching example of mosaic evolution. Different parts of a single complex anatomical system retain different ancestral states in different lineages of the same clade; you cannot pick one extant species to be "the" model.
- **Methodological note on primitivity vs. plesiomorphy.** Authors' explicit defense of tree-independent primitivity criteria — homonomy, subdivision count, simplicity, breadth of distribution — is a useful classroom touchstone for the philosophy-of-character section. Compare with character-optimization papers like Springer & Johnson 2015 (Anguilliformes) or Anderson 2008 (gnathostome jaw muscles) to see two contrasting methodological styles applied to the same kind of question.
- **Howell controversy** as a historiography case study. Howell 1936/1937 reorganized monotreme muscle nomenclature, calling them "extremely aberrant"; Westling 1889 and McKay 1894 had identified the muscles by therian homology. This paper restores Westling/McKay. Demonstrates that even basic descriptive anatomy literature is not stable — naming conventions get revised, and a 21st-century student needs to read across at least three layers of synonymy to use older references.
- **CT-before-dissection** as a workflow worth flagging — bones in natural articulation are recorded before dissection disturbs them, which is increasingly standard for high-value comparative material.
- **Pairs with Reilly & White 2003** (epipubic bones + cross-couplet kinetic linkage in marsupials and tritylodontids) for the broader theme of "non-therian mammalian limb-trunk integration." Pairs with **Allen et al. 2014** (crocodilian limb muscle architecture) and **Meers 2003** (crocodilian forelimb) for the Extant Phylogenetic Bracket logic of inferring extinct-mammaliform forelimb function.

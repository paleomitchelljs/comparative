# Naumann et al. (2017) — Cucullaris and branchial muscle development in the longnose gar

## Citation

Naumann B, Warth P, Olsson L, Konstantinidis P. 2017. The development of the
cucullaris muscle and the branchial musculature in the Longnose Gar (*Lepisosteus
osseus*, Lepisosteiformes, Actinopterygii) and its implications for the evolution of
the head/trunk interface. *Evolution & Development*. doi:10.1111/ede.12239

## Question

Where does the cucullaris come from developmentally, and what does that say about
the head/trunk boundary? The muscle sits across it, and its germ layer origin is
the evidence that decides which side it belongs to.

## Taxa and material

***Lepisosteus osseus***, the longnose gar — a non-teleost actinopterygian, which
is what makes it useful: it samples the fish end of the tree outside the teleost
crown.

## Findings worth carrying

**The cucullaris develops from the same cranial mesoderm population as the
branchial constrictors**, supporting a branchiomeric rather than somitic origin.

That is the developmental evidence under this dataset's `mass: "branchiomeric"` on
the `protractor-pectoralis` record — the muscle whose whole significance is that it
spans the head–trunk boundary and whose innervation (accessory nerve, or its
non-mammalian vagal equivalent) is not spinal.

**They also report that the muscle has been misidentified in earlier work on this
fish**, which is recorded on the row.

## Scored

2 occurrence rows on *Lepisosteus osseus* — `branchial-constrictors` and
`protractor-pectoralis`.

**The row previously named a catfish**, an animal these authors do not examine. It
was re-attributed to the gar, and the correction is the same class as the Werneburg
and Schilling ones: a citation records where a claim was read, not where it was
observed.

## Limitations

- **Developmental, not descriptive.** No attachment rows come from it, and none
  should.
- **One species.** Whether the same holds across Actinopterygii is not tested here.

## Relevance to comparative anatomy teaching

The clean case for using development to settle a homology that topology cannot.
Pair with **Sefton et al. (2016)** on the cucullaris and the head–trunk boundary and
with **Ziermann & Diogo (2019)** on head muscle development across gnathostomes.

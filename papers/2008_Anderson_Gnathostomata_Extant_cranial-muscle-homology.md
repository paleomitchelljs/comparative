# Anderson (2008) — Cranial muscle homology across modern gnathostomes

## Citation

Anderson PSL. 2008. Cranial muscle homology across modern gnathostomes.
*Biological Journal of the Linnean Society* 94: 195–216.

## Question

Written against the same problem as Werneburg (2011): imprecise terminology makes
it impossible to compare cranial musculature between taxa from different
higher-order groups. Anderson's aim is to state homology hypotheses across four
modern gnathostome groups and then test them.

## Taxa and material

Five species across four groups:

| Group | Species |
|---|---|
| Actinopterygii | *Amia calva* |
| Sarcopterygii | *Latimeria chalumnae* |
| Elasmobranchii | *Squalus acanthias*, *Chlamydoselachus anguineus* |
| Holocephali | ***Hydrolagus colliei*** |

Homologies are hypothesised from topological data in the anatomical literature,
**supplemented by his own new observations of *Hydrolagus colliei***, then tested
for congruence against accepted gnathostome phylogeny.

## Findings worth carrying

**Eight muscle groups are unambiguously homologous across all taxa examined.** Four
more are homologous across a majority. **Twelve are hypothesised to be basal across
all gnathostomes.**

That is the kind of result this dataset is organised to hold: a count of how much
of a head can be traced across the whole group, with the residue named rather than
smoothed over.

He also re-identifies at least one *Hydrolagus* muscle that had been misnamed in
earlier work.

## Scored

5 occurrence rows on ***Hydrolagus colliei***, the animal he observed himself —
adductor mandibulae, intermandibularis, hypobranchial muscles, and two scored
`present: "no"`: the interhyoideus and the levator arcus palatini.

The absences are the useful half. A holocephalan has an autostylic jaw suspension,
so the spiracular series has no muscle where an elasmobranch has one, and saying so
is an observation rather than a silence.

`adductor-mandibulae` carries Anderson's **muscle group #1** — the one group
present and unambiguously homologous in all five gnathostomes he compares.

## Limitations

- **Mostly a literature synthesis.** Only *Hydrolagus* is newly observed, and only
  those rows are scored to a species here.
- **A cross-check rather than a row source**, at 3.8 origin/insertion mentions per
  page. Its value is in adjudicating names across the fish end of the tree, not in
  filling attachment rows.

## Relevance to comparative anatomy teaching

The cleanest available statement of how much of the gnathostome head is shared, and
of which groups the shared portion consists. Pair with **Ziermann et al. (2014)**
for hagfish, lamprey and skate, with **Huber et al. (2011)** for the shark, and with
**Werneburg (2011)** for the same nomenclatural problem attacked in a different
clade.

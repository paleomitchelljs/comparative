# Wilga, Hueter, Wainwright & Motta (2001) — Upper jaw protrusion in elasmobranchs

*American Zoologist* 41: 1248–1257. doi:10.1093/icb/41.6.1248
Source key: `wilga-etal-2001`

## Why it was added

To settle when the shark preorbitalis acts and what it does, which the
simulator's *Squalus* model had taken from Huber et al. (2011) and then from a
verbal account. This review brings together the muscle-activity and video work
on four elasmobranchs (*Squalus acanthias*, *Rhinobatos lentiginosus*,
*Negaprion brevirostris*, *Sphyrna tiburo*).

## What it says about the preorbitalis, in the paper's terms

- **When.** Protrusion of the upper jaw occurs as the jaws **close** on the prey.
  In *Squalus*, *Negaprion*, *Rhinobatos* and *Sphyrna* the quadratomandibularis
  and preorbitalis are active, and the upper jaw protrudes, during jaw closure
  (the compressive phase). The paper does not place protrusion in jaw opening.
- **How, in basal sharks.** A single undivided preorbitalis runs from the nasal
  capsule to the quadratomandibularis. It pulls the *posterior* of the upper jaw
  forward, which makes the *anterior* part slide ventrally down a groove in the
  braincase. *Squalus* is given as an animal in which this was shown by motor
  activity recorded with video.
- **The lower jaw.** The quadratomandibularis, which forms the adductor complex
  with the preorbitalis, adducts both jaws and in doing so pulls the upper jaw
  toward the rising lower jaw, effectively protruding it. The authors say this
  works best if the lower jaw is held against large prey.
- **Rays are different.** The ray's lateral preorbitalis inserts on the mandible
  and is believed to lift the lower jaw. It is unlikely to protrude the upper jaw,
  since batoids have no cranial articulation to guide the upper jaw, and they
  protrude it straight down, not forward and down.
- **Levator palatoquadrati.** Active during retraction, never protrusion, in
  *Squalus* and in *Rhinobatos*; its function reverses in carcharhinids, where its
  origin has moved forward.

## What is not in it

Nothing says the preorbitalis acts during jaw *opening*, and nothing describes it
stabilising the upper jaw during closing. Both were offered as the project
owner's account; this paper does not support the first and does not address the
second.

## Mining accounting

Review paper, about 1.6 origin/insertion mentions per page over 12 pages, so not
a descriptive source. Filed for *Squalus acanthias*: the preorbitalis (parked, no
record) and the levator palatoquadrati. Not mined: *Rhinobatos lentiginosus*,
*Negaprion brevirostris* and *Sphyrna tiburo*, none of which has a species
record yet. The source therefore stays `not-started`.

# Ziermann, Miyashita & Diogo 2014 — Cyclostome + chondrichthyan cephalic muscles

## Citation

Ziermann JM, Miyashita T, Diogo R. 2014. Cephalic muscles of Cyclostomes (hagfishes and lampreys) and Chondrichthyes (sharks, rays and holocephalans): comparative anatomy and early evolution of the vertebrate head muscles. *Zoological Journal of the Linnean Society* 172: 771–802. doi:10.1111/zoj.12186.

## Question

Provide the first dissection-based, unified-terminology survey of the cephalic muscles of non-osteichthyan vertebrates, and from it reconstruct the muscular bauplan of the LCA of extant vertebrates and the LCA of gnathostomes. Specifically: which head muscles are cyclostome autapomorphies, which are gnathostome synapomorphies, and how did the cucullaris / head–pectoral-girdle connection evolve? The authors frame this as filling the "long-standing, central issue regarding vertebrate comparative anatomy" of linking head muscles to pectoral girdle evolution.

## Taxa and material

Five species dissected, all under iodine-stain enhancement with a Nikon SMZ-2B scope:

- *Myxine glutinosa* (Atlantic hagfish; 2 juveniles) — Cyclostomata, Myxinoidea
- *Petromyzon marinus* (sea lamprey; 3 spawning-phase females) — Cyclostomata, Petromyzontiformes
- *Hydrolagus colliei* (spotted ratfish; 3 juvenile females) — Chondrichthyes, Holocephali
- *Squalus acanthias* (spiny dogfish; 3 juvenile males) — Chondrichthyes, Elasmobranchii
- *Leucoraja erinacea* (little skate; 3 juvenile females) — Chondrichthyes, Elasmobranchii
- Plus *Mustelus laevis* (common smooth-hound; 1 juvenile) to cross-check Kesteven's 1942–45 dogfish descriptions.

Osteichthyans are used as outgroup comparator via the authors' earlier dissection corpus (Diogo & Abdala 2010 and precursors) rather than dissected fresh here.

## Time frame

Extant, but with explicit ancestral-state reconstruction to LCA of Vertebrata (≈ 430–520 Mya per the authors) and LCA of extant gnathostomes.

## Methods

Gross dissection with iodine contrast; muscle counts tabulated per taxon in Tables 1–4 (one table for each of the four classical Edgeworth muscle groups: mandibular [CNV], hyoid [CNVII], branchial sensu lato [CNIX/X for true branchial + CNXI cucullaris/laryngeal/coracobranchial/epibranchial], and hypobranchial [spinal]). Homology calls rest on (i) innervation, (ii) topology relative to skeletal landmarks, (iii) developmental data from the literature, and (iv) fibre orientation. Where cyclostome vs. gnathostome homology is not secure, the authors "follow the traditional nomenclature for cyclostomes (Marinelli & Strenger 1954, 1956) until the hypothesized homologies are supported by further evidence such as specific gene expression patterns and knockdown phenotypes." Synonym matrix in Appendix S1.

## Findings

1. **Muscle counts per taxon (mandibular / hyoid / branchial / hypobranchial):** *Myxine* 24/4/3/3; *Petromyzon* 26/0/76/1; *Hydrolagus* 7/4/16/2; *Squalus* 5/3/27/4; *Leucoraja* 6/6/28/4. Lampreys and hagfishes carry far more mandibular muscles than any gnathostome; the authors flag this as a "potential synapomorphy of cyclostomes" but concede independent evolution in each lineage cannot be ruled out.

2. **Inferred LCA of extant vertebrates:** an undifferentiated intermandibularis muscle sheet, at least one "other mandibular muscle" (probable precursor of labial muscles), at least one constrictor hyoideus, at least one branchial constrictor and/or interbranchial, and undifferentiated epibranchial + hypobranchial muscle sheets. Cyclostomes and gnathostomes already differ "strikingly" from each other, but share more than Mallatt's (1996, 1997, 2008) "mandibular branchiomere" model assumed.

3. **Inferred LCA of extant gnathostomes:** intermandibularis anterior, intermandibularis posterior, adductor mandibulae A2, levator arcus palatini, at least some labial muscles; interhyoideus, adductor arcus palatini, constrictor hyoideus dorsalis; constrictores branchiales, coracobranchiales, at least some epibranchial muscles, and a **single undivided cucullaris** inserted onto both pectoral girdle and dorsal branchial arches; plus coracomandibularis and sternohyoideus.

4. **Cucullaris is the key macroevolutionary element.** Plesiomorphic state: a single branchial muscle innervated by CNXI, inserting on girdle + arches. In batoids and holocephalans it splits into bundles toward the adult stage. In **osteichthyans** it divides into the protractor pectoralis (to girdle) and levatores arcuum branchialium (to arches) — this division is the authors' proposed osteichthyan synapomorphy. In amniotes protractor pectoralis further differentiates into trapezius + sternocleidomastoideus, finally decoupling head and girdle. "The general evolutionary trend seen in vertebrates towards a greater separation between the head and pectoral girdle later culminated in the separation of the cucullaris into the protractor pectoralis and levatores arcuum branchialium in osteichthyans and finally in the formation of the tetrapod neck." Kesteven's (1942) "levatores arcuum branchialium" as thin structures over branchial arches in selachians, batoids and holocephalans could not be repeated in the authors' *Squalus* or *Mustelus* dissections and is probably part of the cucullaris or the epibranchial musculature — so osteichthyans alone have the true levatores arcuum branchialium.

5. **Mandibular muscles are jaw neomorphs, not rearranged branchial muscles.** "The gnathostome jaw muscles represent neomorphs that evolved along with the jaws." Dlx-dependent dorso-ventral patterning of the mandibular arch patterns both skeleton and muscles in concert (Heude et al. 2010; Medeiros & Crump 2012). The authors reject Mallatt's serial-branchiomere scheme and instead treat the Dlx-dependent musculoskeletal patterning as the shared condition that preceded the origin of the jaw.

6. **Holocephalan "labial" muscles (preorbitalis, levator anguli oris anterior, levator prelabialis, labialis anterior) are not homologous with elasmobranch or osteichthyan labial muscles.** The levator arcus palatini was lost in Holocephali (associated with fusion of palatoquadrate to neurocranium = holostyly). These "upper-lip" muscles are topographically more comparable to lamprey larval buccalis anterior than to elasmobranch preorbitalis. Congruently, CNV2 innervates the labial muscles in holocephalans (primitive cyclostome-like pattern retained? or secondary anterior CNV2 extension?) whereas CNV3 innervates them in elasmobranchs and osteichthyans.

7. **Intermandibularis is a single muscle plesiomorphically,** not divided into anterior/posterior in the LCA of gnathostomes. The apparent "anterior vs. posterior" split in many chondrichthyans is an artifact of the muscles being "blended with one another so much that the anterior cannot be readily distinguished from the posterior." Holocephalan intermandibularis is a single muscle; its attachment to maxillary labial cartilage is secondary.

8. **Cyclostome velar muscles + gnathostome levator arcus palatini (= constrictor dorsalis of Edgeworth) are probably homogenic,** i.e., derive from the same anlage. Supported by shared engrailed immunoactivity in lamprey velothyroideus and zebrafish levator arcus palatini (Holland et al. 1993), with the caveat that "similar gene expression patterns do not necessarily warrant a homology."

9. **Lampreys lack hyoid muscles in the adult.** "No muscle identified by us is innervated by CNVII. Therefore, sea lampreys lack hyoid muscles." (A larval CNVII constrictor prebranchialis has been reported by Miyashita 2012.) Change within the lamprey lineage.

10. **Coracobranchiales remain puzzling.** Four non-exclusive hypotheses: (1) gnathostome neomorphs; (2) homologous with cyclostome interbranchiales (Mallatt 1996); (3) lost in cyclostomes; (4) hypobranchial muscles (Wiley 1979). Present in all gnathostomes here; spinal innervation via the cervical plexus occurs only in chondrichthyans (Edgeworth called that derived; Didier 1995 argued it plesiomorphic within chondrichthyans).

## Key figures and tables

- **Tables 1–4** are the practical deliverable: mandibular, hyoid, branchial, and hypobranchial muscle names for each of the five taxa plus reconstructed LCAs (Cyclostomata, Gnathostomata, Chondrichthyes, Elasmobranchii, Chondrichthyes, Osteichthyes). Use as a cross-clade translation dictionary.
- **Fig. 2 (*Myxine*), Fig. 3 (*Petromyzon*), Fig. 4 (*Hydrolagus*), Fig. 5 (*Squalus*), Fig. 6 (*Leucoraja*)** — each combines iodine-stained dissection photos with a colour-coded schematic (mandibular red/orange/pink, hyoid green, true branchial blue, epibranchial brown, hypobranchial green). These are the cleanest published lateral-view cephalic-muscle maps for these five exemplar species and will translate directly to lecture slides.
- **Appendix S1** is the synonym matrix — maps Marinelli & Strenger, Edgeworth 1935, Miyashita 2012, Mallatt 1996/97/2008, and the present paper's names onto one another.

## Scored from Tables 1 and 2

The tables OCR to collapsed columns and read cleanly as page images at 200 dpi
(PDF pp. 5–8), the same treatment the Diogo tables needed. Read that way they
close the thinnest column in the dataset relative to what rests on it: the
cyclostome outgroup is the polarity anchor for every cranial homology downstream,
and it held three occurrences per lineage.

**Seven occurrences on four arch-1 and arch-2 records.** Four of the seven are
`present: "no"`, which is a claim here and not a blank — the absences are the
whole point.

- **`intermandibularis`** gains both. Table 1 puts an *undifferentiated
  intermandibularis muscle sheet* at the last common ancestor of extant Vertebrata
  and of extant Cyclostomata; *Myxine* has the **tubulatus** in that position and
  *Petromyzon* the **constrictor cornualis superficialis** and **constrictor
  glossae profundus internus**. The intermandibularis proper, divided into anterior
  and posterior, appears at the gnathostome ancestor.
- **Both rows are scored.** The tubulatus arises from the lingual arches and
  surrounds the longitudinalis linguae as a tube, so it gets an origin and no
  insertion. The constrictor cornualis superficialis arises from the **cartilago
  styliformis** and inserts with its contralateral fellow on a **median raphe** —
  which is the comparison worth having, because the shark intermandibularis does
  exactly that on `midventral-raphe`. The ventral midline sheet leaves nothing
  skeletal behind on either side of the origin of jaws.
- **`interhyoideus` is absent in both, and Table 2 is why.** *Petromyzon* is
  credited with **zero** adult hyoid muscles and *Myxine* with four, none of them
  the interhyoideus, which first appears at the gnathostome ancestor. Those two
  rows are what make it a gnathostome character rather than a vertebrate one.
- **`adductor-mandibulae` absent in *Myxine*** — Table 1's row for mandibular
  muscles other than the intermandibularis is empty for both cyclostomes.
- **`levator-arcus-palatini` absent in both, against a named alternative.** The
  velar muscles are the candidate, on the hypothesis that they and the levator
  arcus palatini both derive from the constrictor dorsalis anlage. **These authors
  decline it** — see the limitation below — so the rows are scored absent with the
  hypothesis recorded rather than deleted.

One new skeletal element, `cartilago-styliformis`, so the lamprey's
intermandibularis homologue has a skeletal end to hang on.

**Not yet taken:** the labial, nasal, lingual, dental and velar groups, which are
most of *Myxine*'s 24 and *Petromyzon*'s 26 mandibular muscles. They are
lineage-specific differentiations with no gnathostome counterpart on this paper's
own reading, so they need either new records or a decision that the dataset does
not hold them. Tables 3 and 4, on the branchial and hypobranchial muscles, are
also unread at row level.

## Limitations

Authors are explicit: (a) some muscle homologies across cyclostomes and gnathostomes remain "controversial" and await gene-expression + knockdown confirmation; (b) sampling within cyclostomes is minimal — one species per lineage; (c) juvenile-only specimens for *Hydrolagus*, *Squalus*, and *Leucoraja*; (d) they couldn't locate Kesteven's levatores arcuum branchialium, meaning either the muscles vary intraspecifically or Kesteven's identifications were suspect; (e) the engrailed-based homology of velar muscles and levator arcus palatini is "tentative"; (f) *Petromyzon* velothyroideus is larval and whether it persists into the adult as the depressor veli remains unresolved.

## Relevance to comparative anatomy teaching

Paper is the best single source for three lectures. First, a **cyclostome head-muscle survey** lecture: unlike the usual focus on the axial/hypaxial body plan of lamprey and hagfish, Figures 2 and 3 give colour-coded views of the cephalic muscles and force students to confront that the lamprey head has dozens of named muscles with unique names (annularis, copuloglossus, basilariglossus, stylotectalis) that do not map cleanly onto gnathostome muscles. Good antidote to the tendency to teach cyclostomes as just "things that lack a jaw." Second, a **cucullaris/neck-origin** lecture: the authors' hypothesis — single undivided cucullaris → protractor pectoralis + levatores arcuum branchialium in osteichthyans → trapezius + sternocleidomastoideus in amniotes — is a clean narrative for the evolution of the tetrapod neck and pairs naturally with Ericsson, Knight & Johanson 2013 on the trapezius/spine-of-scapula connection. Third, a **homology methodology** lecture: the paper walks through how the authors use topology + innervation + development + fibre direction together, and where each criterion fails (Luther 1914's trigeminal-branch homology criterion was unreliable; engrailed expression is suggestive but not conclusive; Kesteven's 1942 descriptions cannot be repeated). Tables 1–4 are the go-to cross-clade head-muscle dictionary for any student working across these taxa.

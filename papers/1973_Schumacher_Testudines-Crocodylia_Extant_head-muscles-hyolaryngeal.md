# Schumacher (1973) — Head muscles and hyolaryngeal skeleton of turtles and crocodilians

## Citation

Schumacher G-H. 1973. The head muscles and hyolaryngeal skeleton of turtles and
crocodilians. *Biology of the Reptilia* 4 (Morphology D), Academic Press, pp.
101–199.

## What it is

Turtle and crocodylian cranial myology in one source, drawing on roughly **forty
chelonian species** — the species list runs family by family through Chelydridae,
Kinosterninae, Dermatemydidae, Testudinidae and the rest — and synthesising them
against earlier work by Ogushi (1913), Sewertzoff (1929), Graper (1932),
Gnanamuthu (1937), Hacker & Schumacher (1955) and Sondhi (1958), plus his own
observations.

## The species problem, which is the reason to read this note first

**It is written as a clade-level synthesis, not as a species description.** The
form of a statement is "in all turtles X, and in *Emys*, *Testudo* and the
trionychids Y". He examined *Pseudemys scripta* among many others, but the text
does not report one animal.

So its turtle rows sit on **`testudines-generalised`** with `speciesBasis:
"generalised"`, for the same reason Werneburg's do. **This source cannot close the
species-level turtle cranial gap**, however many species stand behind it, because
it never says which animal a given sentence describes.

That is not a criticism of the paper — a synthesis is what it set out to be. It is
a statement about what can honestly be scored from it.

## Scored

3 occurrence rows: the adductor mandibulae internus and posterior on
`testudines-generalised`, and the internus on *Alligator mississippiensis*.

Schumacher treats the internus of turtles and crocodilians as a **complex rather
than a muscle** — a pseudotemporalis plus a pterygoideus, the latter further split
into anterior and posterior parts in crocodilians. The intramandibularis is what
makes the group unusual: a division of the adductor complex that lies within the
lower jaw.

## Limitations

- **No species-level turtle rows are possible from it.** See above.
- **The hyolaryngeal half is unmined.** The paper covers the hyoid and larynx as
  well as the jaw muscles, and this dataset has taken only the adductor complex.

## Relevance to comparative anatomy teaching

The one source in the corpus that puts the turtle and crocodylian head side by side
under one nomenclature, which is exactly the comparison a student needs when the
two clades' jaw muscles are named differently in every other paper. Pair with
**Werneburg (2011)** for the turtle alone at much finer resolution, and with
**Haas (1973)** — the same *Biology of the Reptilia* volume — for the lepidosaurs.

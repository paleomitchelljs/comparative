# Sigurdsen, Green & Bishop 2012 — Did *Triadobatrachus* jump? Early salientian forelimb + pectoral girdle

**Citation.** Sigurdsen, T., Green, D. M. & Bishop, P. J. 2012. Did *Triadobatrachus* jump? Morphology and evolution of the anuran forelimb in relation to locomotion in early salientians. *Fieldiana Life and Earth Sciences* 5: 77–89. DOI: 10.3158/2158-5520-5.1.77.

## Question

Did the Lower Triassic stem anurans *Triadobatrachus massinoti* and *Czatkobatrachus polonicus* **jump**? Hind-limb evidence has been equivocal; this paper examines the **forelimb + pectoral girdle** — the part that absorbs the landing force in modern ranid jumps — to see whether early salientians had the muscular-architectural signature of saltation.

> "The Lower Triassic amphibians *Triadobatrachus massinoti* and *Czatkobatrachus polonicus* are universally regarded as stem anurans. However, there is still uncertainty about whether or not they were capable of jumping like true anurans as their postcranial features have so far provided only equivocal evidence. Although previous work has concentrated on the anatomy of the hind limb, here we examine the anatomy of the forelimb, comparing stem and crown-group anurans to other amphibians."

## Taxa + material

- **Stem anurans:** *Triadobatrachus massinoti* (Lower Triassic, Madagascar, MNHN MAE 126 — high-definition casts examined; single specimen). *Czatkobatrachus polonicus* (Lower Triassic, Poland, described by Evans & Borsuk-Białynicka 1998, 2009). *Prosalirus bitis* (Lower Jurassic, Kayenta Formation, Arizona, MNA V 8725).
- **Stem caecilian:** *Eocaecilia micropodia* (MCZ 9163, casts, Early Jurassic).
- **Proto-lissamphibian:** *Amphibamus grandiceps* + *Doleserpeton annectens* (casts, Lower Carboniferous Mazon Creek / Lower Permian).
- **Extant anurans dissected + compared (all Redpath Museum specimens unless noted):** *Lithobates pipiens* (Ranidae, RM 2812), *L. catesbeianus* (RM 2785); *Anaxyrus americanus* (Bufonidae, RM 4999); *Ascaphus truei* (Ascaphidae, RM 4430); *Leiopelma hochstetteri* (Leiopelmatidae, MCZ 2215), *L. archeyi* (RM 2193–2195); *Xenopus laevis* (Pipidae, RM 2230); *Scaphiopus holbrookii* (Pelobatidae, RM 2425); *Conraua goliath* (Petropedetidae).
- **Extant salamanders for outgroup muscle nomenclature:** *Hynobius nigrescens* (Hynobiidae, MCZ 22553); *Andrias davidianus* (Cryptobranchidae, FMNH 166872); *Ambystoma tigrinum* (Ambystomatidae, RM 2161).
- No specimens of "running frogs" (*Kassina*-type) examined — acknowledged gap.

## Time frame

Lower Carboniferous (proto-lissamphibian *Amphibamus*) → Lower Permian (*Doleserpeton*) → Lower Triassic (*Triadobatrachus*, *Czatkobatrachus*) → Lower Jurassic (*Prosalirus*, *Vieraella*, *Eocaecilia*) → Middle Jurassic (*Notobatrachus*) → Extant. Time field: Triassic-Extant.

## Methods

- High-definition casts of *Triadobatrachus* examined; original photographs + observations provided by J.-C. Rage (MNHN).
- Fossil and extant tetrapod specimens examined at MCZ, FMNH, RM.
- Ethanol-preserved anuran + salamander specimens **dissected** with focus on thorax + forelimb musculature; homologies cross-referenced to Gaupp 1904, Francis 1934, Ritland 1955a,b, Duellman & Trueb 1994, Walthall & Ashley-Ross 2006, Abdala & Diogo 2010.
- Characters compared across Lissamphibia using Table 1: number of presacral vertebrae, tail/caudal vertebrae, iliac-blade orientation, epipodial fusion, femur:humerus length ratio, deltopectoral-crest orientation, medial manual torsion. Nine taxa (*Doleserpeton*, *Eocaecilia*, Caudata, *Triadobatrachus*, *Czatkobatrachus*, *Prosalirus*, *Vieraella*, *Notobatrachus*, extant anurans *Ascaphus*, *Scaphiopus*, *Lithobates*).
- No new phylogenetic analysis; uses Frost et al. 2006 + standard lissamphibian framework.

## Findings

1. **Modern-anuran forelimb signature — what to look for on *Triadobatrachus*.** The humerus has a sigmoid shaft curvature with strong dorsal curvature toward the head and ventral curvature near the distal end. The **deltopectoral crest (crista ventralis)** is long (~½ humerus length in strong jumpers such as *Lithobates catesbeianus*), deflected **laterally** (giving the proximal humerus a concave lateral face), and carries the pectoralis + deltoid origins/insertions. The radius and ulna are **fused** (radio-ulna). The manus is **strongly medially twisted** so digits point medially. Carpal fusions (proximal elements fused to a radiale + ulnare-intermedium). Phalangeal formula 2-2-3-3 (digit 1 lost). The scapula has an **anterior process** between dorsal + ventral expansions serving as deltoid origin.

2. **Salamander / primitive tetrapod forelimb baseline.** Salamanders show (a) unfused radius + ulna, (b) deltopectoral crest with ventral edge curving **medially + ventrally** — creating a medial/ventro-medial concavity near the proximal humerus, as in most sprawling tetrapods, (c) absence of medial manus rotation, (d) scapulocoracoid as a single element pierced by two foramina, (e) a separate **dorsal process (crista dorsalis)** on the humerus for *m. subscapularis* insertion (absent in frogs). The anuran condition is strongly derived from this.

3. **Deltoid enlargement is the key anuran muscular apomorphy** (Fig. 6 is the muscle-mapping synthesis). In salamanders the deltoid (= procoracohumeralis) is a single slip originating on the procoracoid and inserting on the crista ventralis of the humerus. In frogs the deltoid is subdivided into multiple bellies (three in Gaupp's 1904 scheme: pars episternalis, clavicularis, scapularis), considerably thicker than surrounding muscles, and in *Rana* + *Lithobates* is "the most powerful of the muscles originating on the trunk and inserting on the forelimb." The enlarged deltoid correlates with the **enlarged, laterally-deflected deltopectoral crest** — the bone-remodelling signature of powerful forelimb protraction.

4. ***Triadobatrachus* humerus diagnostics** (Fig. 7). About 17.5 mm long as preserved, **sigmoidal** curvature (like modern frogs + Jurassic *Notobatrachus*), **no dorsal process** (anuran-like, salamander-unlike), no ossified capitulum (large gap at distal end lateral to entepicondyle — same in *Czatkobatrachus*), distal + proximal ends poorly ossified (cartilage caps unpreserved). Deltopectoral crest **tapers distally and disappears at mid-shaft** (preserved ~8 mm), is **deflected laterally** so the proximal humerus is lateral-concave — "as in true jumping anurans." The raised area on the medial side near the ventral edge is interpreted as the pectoralis insertion (modern pipid configuration). Radio-ulna **unfused** but close in shape to salamanders. Carpus preserves separately ossified radiale, ulnare, intermedium — primitive carpal condition.

5. ***Czatkobatrachus*** differs subtly: deltopectoral crest leans slightly **medially** (side of the pectoralis insertion), with a muscle scar for the deltoid on the **lateral** side — essentially mirror-image of *Triadobatrachus*. Bone is more slender and **more highly ossified**, olecranon well formed. Scapulocoracoid less clearly single-element. These are morphological + presumably behavioural differences between the two Triassic stem-anurans — early salientian diversity.

6. **Medial manus rotation present in *Prosalirus* by the Early Jurassic.** Already present (Shubin & Jenkins 1995; Jenkins & Shubin 1998). By this stage the pectoral girdle was essentially modern anuran (separate scapula + coracoid). In *Triadobatrachus* + *Czatkobatrachus* medial rotation is not demonstrable, but neither is it excluded — preservation precludes the call. *Vieraella* (Early Jurassic, Patagonia) has medially-rotated manus; *Notobatrachus* (Middle Jurassic, Patagonia) lacks it — reversal.

7. **Basal living anurans (*Ascaphus*, *Leiopelma*) are not primitive models for jumping.** *Ascaphus* lands on its belly or head after short leaps and does not use the forelimbs; *Leiopelma* rotates the forelimbs anteriorly on takeoff and lands on forefeet (RM + PJB observations). *Leiopelma* is the closer match to the *Triadobatrachus* condition. The authors conclude (reinforcing Ritland 1955b + Essner et al. 2010 + Jenkins & Shubin 1998) that **the *Ascaphus* pattern is derived, not primitive**.

8. **Central conclusion.** *Triadobatrachus* forelimb has (a) laterally deflected deltopectoral crest, (b) lateral concavity on proximal humerus, (c) sigmoid humeral curvature, (d) enlarged deltoid insertion zone, (e) scapula with anteriorly protruding process serving as deltoid origin — all anuran jumping-signature features. Absent: radio-ulna fusion, medial manus rotation, anterior iliac blade, epipodial fusion, long femur:humerus ratio (*Triadobatrachus* = 0.85; *Lithobates* = 1.80). Diagnosis: "*Triadobatrachus* was certainly not so capable of long jumps as some modern anurans, yet its anatomy does suggest that jumping or hopping was part of its locomotor repertoire." Consistent with **salamander-like crawling + short hops**, similar to *Leiopelma archeyi* today. The deltoid-enlargement heterochrony started before the radio-ulna + ilio-sacral + manus changes, not after.

9. **Broader lissamphibian context.** Forelimb of *Eocaecilia* (Early Jurassic stem caecilian) + *Doleserpeton* (Lower Permian amphibamid) are **remarkably similar** to that of salamanders (Sigurdsen & Bolt 2009, 2010). Salamander forelimb approximates the primitive lissamphibian condition. Stem anurans diverged from this baseline **primarily through pectoralis + deltoid enlargement reshaping the deltopectoral crest**. The radio-ulna fusion, iliac reorientation, and medial manus rotation stack on top of this foundation.

## Limitations

- Single specimen of *Triadobatrachus* (MNHN MAE 126), known only as natural mold + casts. Many diagnostic features are inferable only from preserved contours. The specimen may be immature — could bias ossification-based interpretations.
- Cartilage caps + capitulum unpreserved → distal humerus characters unscorable.
- "Jumping" vs. "hopping" vs. "saltation" terminology not operationalized; qualitative assessment rather than biomechanical simulation.
- Phylogeny dependency: Evans & Borsuk-Białynicka 1998 place *Ascaphus* + *Leiopelma* separately (Ascaphidae + Leiopelmatidae); Frost et al. 2006 lump them in Leiopelmatidae. Character-polarity conclusions for *Ascaphus* behavior change depending on this.
- No "running frog" (*Kassina*-type) examined — one of Emerson's 1978 locomotor modes is absent from the sample.
- Iliac blade orientation + presacral vertebra count for *Triadobatrachus* shown in Table 1 as "posterior" / "14" but Evans & Borsuk-Białynicka 2009 argued for anterior reorientation. The authors mark this with footnotes.
- Deltopectoral crest orientation disagreement between the authors' observations (lateral) and Table 1's "medial" entry for *Triadobatrachus* — not reconciled in text.

## Relevance to comparative anatomy teaching

Single best paper for teaching **how forelimb anatomy, not just hindlimb + pelvis, diagnoses anuran locomotor mode**. Pair with:

- **Prikryl et al. 2009** (anuran pelvic + thigh musculature, *J. Anatomy*) for the complementary hindlimb side.
- **Collings & Richards 2019** (anuran pelvis + hindlimb diceCT — in library as `2019_Collings-Richards_Anura-Hyperoliidae_Extant_pelvis-hindlimb-DICECT.pdf`).
- **Schoch 2014** (amphibian skull evolution — in library) — complementary story of lissamphibian postcranial + cranial remodelling from temnospondyl baseline.
- **Jenkins & Shubin 1998** *Prosalirus* + **Shubin & Jenkins 1995** — the canonical hindlimb-based anuran-origin papers that this one complements.
- **Sigurdsen & Bolt 2009** (lissamphibian humerus elbow origin) — the authors' earlier companion paper, already in library as `2009_Sigurdsen-Bolt_Lissamphibia_Permian-Jurassic_humerus-elbow.pdf`.
- **Ahlberg 2018** (tetrapod origin review — in library) for the deeper context of limb-to-body-plan changes at the fish→tetrapod transition.
- **Abdala & Diogo 2010** (pectoral + forelimb muscle comparative review across tetrapods — in library as `2010_Abdala-Diogo_Tetrapoda_Extant_pectoral-forelimb-muscles.pdf`) for the muscle-name homology matrix used here.

**Pedagogic headline.** Look at the deltopectoral crest orientation before asking "did this animal jump." A laterally-deflected crest means a large deltoid for powerful forelimb protraction; a medially-concave crest means a sprawling posture. *Triadobatrachus* crosses into anuran territory at the crest already, without yet having fused radio-ulna, medially-twisted manus, or anterior ilium. Jumping behavior is a package that assembles part by part across the Triassic–Jurassic, not a single switch.

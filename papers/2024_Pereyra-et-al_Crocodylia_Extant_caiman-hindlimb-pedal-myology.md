# Pereyra et al. (2024) — Hindlimb and pelvic myology of *Caiman yacare*

## Citation

Pereyra ME, Cerroni MA, Lecuona A, Bona P, Fernández Dumont ML, Otero A. 2024.
Hindlimb and pelvic anatomy of *Caiman yacare* (Archosauria, Pseudosuchia): myology
and osteological correlates. *Journal of Anatomy* 244: 749–791.

**Fetched from PubMed Central (green open access); no local PDF is held.** This note
is written from the source record and the rows it supports rather than from a local

## What it gave

Crocodylian crural and pedal myology described **in seven layers**, with
osteological correlates.

The result worth carrying is a first: **the authors identify bony correlates for
pedal muscles**, which had not been done for a crocodylian — fine striations on the
metatarsal surfaces. They also report that **most correlates vary little between
crocodylian species**, which is the condition that makes a correlate usable on a
fossil. A correlate that varies within a clade cannot be read backwards.

## Scored

11 occurrence rows across *Caiman yacare* and *Alligator mississippiensis* —
iliofibularis, gastrocnemius, tibialis anterior, extensor digitorum longus, the
fibularis group and their neighbours in the leg and foot.

This source took Crocodylia from nothing in the leg and foot to a scored column.

One row carries a cross-limb correction: attachment rows on the

## Limitations

- **No local copy.** See above.
- **One species described.** The claim that correlates vary little between
  crocodylian species is the paper's, drawn from comparison; the description itself
  is *Caiman yacare*.

## Relevance to comparative anatomy teaching

The pedal counterpart to the correlate work on the forelimb, and the paper that made
crocodylian feet legible to palaeontologists. Pair with **Hattori & Tsuihiji (2021)**
for the same region across all four extant sauropsid clades, and with **Allen et al.
(2021)** for what the resulting attachments are used to compute.

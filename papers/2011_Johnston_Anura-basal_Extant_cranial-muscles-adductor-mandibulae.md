# Johnston 2011 — basal anuran cranial muscles and the folded-sheet model of the jaw adductors

## Citation

Johnston P. 2011. Cranial muscles of the anurans *Leiopelma hochstetteri* and *Ascaphus truei* and the homologies of the mandibular adductors in Lissamphibia and other gnathostomes. *Journal of Morphology* 272: 1492–1512. DOI: 10.1002/jmor.10998.

## Question

The cranial muscles of the two most basal extant anuran lineages had never been described in full. Beyond filling that gap, the paper attacks a standing controversy: how do the jaw adductor muscles of Lissamphibia correspond to those of other gnathostomes? The older literature keyed homology to **where trigeminal nerve branches run relative to the muscle bellies**, and that criterion does not survive comparison across gnathostomes.

## Taxa and material

Dissection of *Leiopelma hochstetteri* (4 specimens) and *Ascaphus truei* (2). Comparative material spans holocephalans (*Callorhinchus milii*), sharks, teleosts (*Polypterus* and others), coelacanth, lungfish, a salamander (*Siren lacertina*), an "advanced" frog (*Rana esculenta*), and caecilians.

## The folded-sheet model — the paper's main contribution

The jaw adductor group is a **continuous sheet**, not a set of independent muscles. Its insertion on the mandible forms an **inverted U** wrapped around the insertion of the m. adductor mandibulae posterior. The components — **internus, longus, externus, lateralis** — are identified by *where they insert into that U*.

Two consequences:

1. **The adductor mandibulae posterior is of different homology from the rest of the group.** It is the axis the sheet folds around, not one of the limbs. Separating it is what makes the rest resolvable.
2. **Insertion position replaces trigeminal branch position as the homology criterion.** Johnston argues explicitly that the folded-sheet geometry "represented a better basis for understanding homology than the traditional system based on position of trigeminal nerve branches within the muscle group."

That second point is worth dwelling on for this project: for the jaw adductors, an *attachment-based* criterion outperforms an innervation-based one. It is the opposite of the situation in the limb, where innervation is the stable signal and attachment is labile.

## Other findings

- The model was tested across gnathostomes and found consistent from chondrichthyans to mammals.
- Consistent with the expression domain of the homeobox gene *engrailed 2*, which had not previously been treated as indicative of homology here.
- **The main jaw adductor of teleost fish is not homologous with that of tetrapods** under this system.
- A new muscle is defined in *Ascaphus*: **m. levator anguli oris**. It is absent in *Leiopelma*.
- The **pterygoideus** is a separated component of the internus, not an independent muscle.
- The interpretation of lungfish and coelacanth anatomy differs from Diogo et al. (2008) and Diogo & Abdala (2011) — worth flagging, since this project leans on Diogo's work elsewhere.
- Terminology: "adductor mandibulae" is preferred to "levator mandibulae" for anurans, to align with usage across gnathostomes.
- The internus in *Leiopelma* has a rostral part inserting by flat tendon immediately medial to the quadrate–mandibular articulation, and a caudal part; its rostral origin on the dorsal braincase extends further forward than in *Ascaphus* or most other frogs.

## Limitations

- Two species of frog, both from lineages chosen for being basal rather than representative.
- The *Ascaphus*/*Leiopelma* difference in V3 penetration of the adductors is presented as a character for future cladistic analysis, not as a resolved polarity.
- The claim that teleost and tetrapod main adductors are non-homologous is a substantial departure from common usage and rests on the folded-sheet reading.

## Relevance to this project

This is the source for splitting the single `adductor-mandibulae` record into its components. The dataset now carries `adductor-mandibulae-internus`, `-longus`, `-externus`, `-lateralis`, `-posterior` and `levator-anguli-oris`, each with the folded-sheet rationale, with the parent record retained as the complex.

It also supplies a genuinely useful counterweight to the project's general line that innervation beats topology: in the jaw adductors, insertion geometry is the better criterion. Both claims are about which evidence is *stable* in a given system, not about a universal ranking of criteria.

## Attachment pass (August 2026)

Scored on a computed worklist rather than the hand-assembled one: ranking every
source by *cited-but-unscored rows × specific-attachment density* put Johnston
fourth, and separated him cleanly from the surveys above him — 43 specific
attachment statements against 8 hedged ones, where Abdala & Diogo (2010) is 49
against 157.

**What was scored.** The *Leiopelma* group row, as the union of the components
Johnston traces through the continuous block: origins on the dorsal braincase,
parietal, otic capsule, crista parotica, squamosal and quadrate; insertions on
the medial mandible (internus, two parts), the coronoid area at the apex of the
U (longus), the lateral limb of the U (externus) and the dorsal mandible rostral
to the articulation (posterior).

**What was deliberately not scored, and why it is the more useful half.**

- *The lateralis insertion.* Johnston gives its origin on the quadrate and its
  course lateral to V3, and never says where it ends.
- *Every Ascaphus component.* He describes *Ascaphus* as similar in general
  configuration and then names only the differences — internus origin less
  rostral, adductor posterior separate, V3 in a different position. **"Similar to
  Leiopelma" is a statement of resemblance, not a second set of observations.**
  Copying the six *Leiopelma* rows across would have manufactured six attachments
  out of one sentence and moved the coverage figure by more than the evidence
  moved. The *Ascaphus* group row carries only the braincase origin and the
  mandibular insertion; the four component rows carry a note saying why they are
  empty.
- *Callorhinchus milii.* Confirmed as genuine comparative material (NMNZ P29678),
  so the citation is sound — but the detailed accounts in the comparative section
  are for *Cephaloscyllium*, *Latimeria* and *Neoceratodus*, not for the
  holocephalan. Nothing to score.

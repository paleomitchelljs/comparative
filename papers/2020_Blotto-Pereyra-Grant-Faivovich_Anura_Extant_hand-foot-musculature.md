# Blotto, Pereyra, Grant & Faivovich (2020) — Hand and foot musculature of Anura

## Citation

Blotto BL, Pereyra MO, Grant T, Faivovich J. 2020. Hand and foot musculature of
Anura: structure, homology, terminology, and synapomorphies for major clades.
*Bulletin of the American Museum of Natural History* 443: 1–156.

## Question

Every previous study of anuran autopod myology was restricted taxonomically or
anatomically, and none had considered the diversity across Anura as a whole. What
does the hand and foot musculature look like when you sample the order properly,
and what does it say about anuran phylogeny?

## Taxa and material

**155 species, representing 46 of the 54 currently recognised families and main
clades.** The descriptive core is one arboreal species, the hylid ***Triprion
petasatus***, described in full as the model for comparison — muscle by muscle,
with the layers they are arranged in and the functions attributed to each.

From that sample they define **20 characters** and optimise them on the current
inclusive phylogeny of Anura.

## Findings worth carrying

**Synapomorphies for seventeen named clades**, including non-homoplastic ones for
clades that had been supported only by molecular data and a few conflicting
phenotypic characters — Acosmanura, Anomocoela and Neobatrachia among them. Muscle
characters doing systematic work is the strongest available argument that autopod
myology is worth scoring at this resolution.

**They address the anuran–caudate homology controversies directly**, in the
context of putative synapomorphies for Ascaphidae + Leiopelmatidae and its sister
clade Lalagobatrachia. That is the tetrapod-wide question this dataset's hand and
foot records sit inside, and it is why the source carries `homologyScope: true`.

**The prepollex and prehallux carry muscles here.** Anurans lost digit I, and the
preaxial muscles that would serve it attach to the prepollex instead — the
position-versus-identity problem with a bone attached to it rather than just a
digit number. Nine autopod elements were added to `skeleton.json` from this source,
the prepollex and prehallux among them. Worth reading beside the Gambaryan note,
where monotremes turn out to have a prepollex too *without* having lost digit I,
which makes the element a preaxial lever rather than a compensation.

## Scored

13 occurrence rows on *Triprion petasatus*, hand and foot, most with attachments.
The hand and foot columns for Anura went from nothing to their present state on
this source.

## Limitations, and why it is only partly mined

**This is a 156-page monograph with its own revised nomenclature and active
disagreements with Abdala & Diogo (2010).** Its terminology reappraisal is a
substantial section in its own right. Scoring the rest of it properly means a
dedicated bridging pass — reconciling two naming schemes across the whole autopod
— rather than a quick one, and `WORKLIST.md` lists it accordingly.

**One species is described in full; the other 154 are scored for characters.** So
the descriptive rows here are *Triprion*, and the clade-level statements are
character optimisations rather than observations of an animal. Do not score the
latter as occurrence rows.

## Relevance to comparative anatomy teaching

The paper to reach for when a student asks whether muscle anatomy carries
phylogenetic signal. It does, and here it recovers clades that molecules found and
that other morphology contradicted. Pair with **Prikryl et al. (2009)** for anuran
pelvic and thigh musculature, and with **Diogo & Molnar (2014)** for the twelve
human hindlimb muscle names that anuran authors apply to non-homologous muscles —
the naming problem this monograph's terminology section is trying to end.

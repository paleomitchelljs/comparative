# Diogo, Johnston, Molnar & Esteve-Altava 2016 — tetrapod limb muscle phenotype emerged >400 MYA

## Citation

Diogo R, Johnston P, Molnar JL, Esteve-Altava B. 2016. Characteristic tetrapod musculoskeletal limb phenotype emerged more than 400 MYA in basal lobe-finned fishes. *Scientific Reports* 6: 37592. DOI: 10.1038/srep37592. **Open access, CC-BY 4.0.**

## Question

Did the tetrapod limb muscle configuration arise suddenly after the dipnoan–tetrapod split, or gradually and much earlier? The standard account has a jump from "two muscle masses (adductor/abductor)" in fish to "more than 50 muscles" in tetrapod limbs. The authors test this by dissecting the closest living relatives of tetrapods and applying Extant Phylogenetic Bracketing — which, they note, had **never** been used for the fins-to-limbs transition despite the relationships being long settled.

## Taxa and material

- *Latimeria chalumnae* (coelacanth) — one formalin-fixed adult (SZ 10378 / CCC161), dissected at Tübingen; plus serial histological sections of a *Latimeria* embryo (CCC162.11) including pectoral fin and girdle
- *Neoceratodus forsteri* (lungfish) — two formalin-fixed adults (JVM-I-1051NC, JVM-I-1052NC), Macquarie University
- *Polypterus senegalus* — three frozen adults, dissected; *P. delhezi* (JVM-I-49PD) micro-CT
- MRI scans of *Latimeria* and *Neoceratodus* from the Digital Fish Library, UCSD
- *Ambystoma* data from the authors' prior work
- *Squalus* as chondrichthyan outgroup

Methods: dissection under magnification, MRI, micro-CT, histology, 3D reconstruction in Amira.

## Time frame

Extant taxa, used to infer the Silurian LCA of extant sarcopterygians (>400 Mya).

## Homology criteria used

Four explicit standards: (1) **positional equivalence**, from bony attachments; (2) **special quality**, from fibre orientation and innervation; (3) **transition**, from palaeontological/developmental intermediates; (4) **congruence**, applying the first three to adjacent muscles and to both dorsal/ventral sides and both appendages.

## Findings

1. **The ancestral gnathostome fin had five muscles, not two.** LCA of extant gnathostomes: ventrally *abductor superficialis*, *abductor profundus*, and the preaxial *pterygialis cranialis*; dorsally *adductor superficialis*, *adductor profundus*.

2. **The ancestral bony fish added a sixth.** LCA of Osteichthyes adds the postaxial *pterygialis caudalis*, present in both appendages of *Polypterus* and *Latimeria*. In *Polypterus* PECA this is Wilhelm et al.'s "zonopropterygialis"; the pterygialis cranialis is their "coracometapterygialis I and II".

3. **The LCA of extant sarcopterygians already had the basic tetrapod limb phenotype — except the autopod.** At least two layers of adductor and abductor muscles, partially segmented proximo-distally at each joint level. The deep musculature (adductor profundus dorsally, abductor profundus ventrally) gave rise to the pronator and supinator series.

4. **Muscle counts across the transition** (pectoral + pelvic = total):
   - *Squalus* (chondrichthyan): 5 + 5 = **10**
   - *Polypterus* (basal actinopterygian): 6 + 6 = **12**
   - *Latimeria* (coelacanth): 20 + 15 = **35**
   - *Neoceratodus* (lungfish): 5 + 25 = **30**
   - *Ambystoma* (salamander): 48 + 59 = **107**; excluding intrinsic autopod muscles, 28 + 27 = **55**

   The punchline: *Polypterus* → *Latimeria* is a jump of 23; *Latimeria* → *Ambystoma* (autopod excluded) is only 20. **The bigger change happened before the sarcopterygian LCA, not after it.**

5. **The dipnoan pectoral fin is secondarily simplified.** *Neoceratodus* has only 5 pectoral muscles but 25 pelvic. This is a dipnoan autapomorphy — which is exactly why excluding *Latimeria* from fins-to-limbs discussions (on the grounds that dipnoans are closer to tetrapods) has misled the field.

6. **Two similarity bottlenecks, not one.** The known bottleneck at the tetrapod node is preceded by an earlier one at the sarcopterygian node. The sarcopterygian LCA had "essentially eight copies of the same highly complex configuration" — dorsal ≈ ventral within each fin, and pectoral ≈ pelvic. In *Latimeria*, 14 PECA muscles have one-to-one topological correspondences with PELA muscles.

7. **Girdle muscles stayed different; distal muscles converged.** Pectoral↔pelvic correspondences among girdle/stylopod muscles are group-to-group without one-to-one equivalence, in both salamanders and humans; zeugopod/autopod correspondences are one-to-one. Pairs with Diogo & Molnar 2014 and Diogo & Ziermann 2015.

8. **The autopod is a genuine novelty.** No intrinsic autopodial muscle of tetrapods is present as a distinct structure in extant sarcopterygian fish. Supported developmentally: the distal tendon primordium appears later than proximal/intermediate primordia, segregates by a different two-step process, develops in spatial isolation from its muscles, and expresses *six-1*, *six-2* and *cek-8* which proximal/intermediate tendons do not.

9. **Contradicts the "one joint" assumption in fossil reconstruction.** Bishop's (2014) reconstruction of stem-tetrapod shoulder/arm/forearm muscles assumed muscles ancestrally did not cross more than one joint. This paper revives Gadow's (1882) position: muscles ran from axial skeleton/girdle all the way to the distal fin and became proximo-distally *partitioned* at the joints during the transition. Biomechanically this makes sense — segmented single-joint muscles are only effective once the fin skeleton is elongated and segmented, as in lobe-fins but not other fishes.

## Specific homology hypotheses (the operational output)

| Fish muscle | Tetrapod derivative(s) | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| abductor superficialis | pectoralis, flexor digitorum communis, flexor antebrachii et carpi ulnaris, coracobrachialis — all developmentally ventral superficial muscles | *Ambystoma* development |
| abductor profundus | supracoracoideus | Second-most-superficial ventral pectoral muscle in both; in the dipnoan the resemblance in attachment, fibre orientation and shape is "strikingly similar" |
| pterygialis cranialis (preaxial) | forelimb: humeroantebrachialis + flexor antebrachii et carpi radialis; hindlimb: ischioflexorius (incl. flexor cruris et tarsi tibialis) | Preaxial position + ventral mass derivation; the FACR differentiates from a different primordium than the postaxial FACU and FDC |
| pterygialis caudalis (postaxial) | pectoral: part of triceps + extensor antebrachii et carpi ulnaris; pelvic: tenuissimus + extensor cruris et tarsi fibularis | Postaxial position + dorsal mass derivation |
| adductor superficialis | dorsal superficial muscles running girdle-to-autopodium | Topology + Gadow's partitioning scenario |
| adductor profundus | the pronator series | Deep dorsal position |
| supinator 1 (*Latimeria*) | coracoradialis | Most proximal deep ventral muscle in both; girdle → along humerus → radius |
| retractor lateralis ventralis pectoralis | serratus anterior + levator scapulae | The only primaxial muscles connecting axial skeleton to pectoral girdle in either taxon |
| levator lateralis (*Latimeria*) / abductor dorsolateralis (*Neoceratodus*) / caudofemoralis (*Ambystoma*) | **NOT homologous to each other** | All abaxial/primaxial and axially attached, but levator lateralis is dorsal musculature while caudofemoralis is ventral |
| — | intrinsic autopodial muscles | **No fish homologue.** Most speculative area; future fossil work might link some supinators/pronators |

Palaeontological check: the *Eusthenopteron* humerus bears osteological correlates of a muscle corresponding to *Latimeria* pronator 1.

## Limitations

- One *Latimeria* specimen, two *Neoceratodus*. Sample sizes are what museum access allows.
- The authors concede "an entire book could be written" on the evidence per muscle; the SI carries the detail, the main text summarises.
- Autopodial homologies are explicitly flagged as the most speculative.
- *Neoceratodus* pectoral simplification is inferred as derived, which is load-bearing for the whole argument.

## Relevance to this project

Fills the two thinnest columns in the dataset. Before this paper the Dipnoi column had **zero** records and there was no coelacanth column at all. It also supplies the ancestral fin muscle set that the entire tetrapod appendicular dataset is a subdivision of — which is the natural root for a drill-down hierarchy (see `docs/ROADMAP.md`).

The four-criteria homology standard is worth quoting directly when teaching what a homology claim requires.

# Molnar, Diogo, Hutchinson & Pierce 2018 — Pectoral muscle evolution across fins-to-limbs

**Citation.** Molnar JL, Diogo R, Hutchinson JR, Pierce SE. 2018. Reconstructing pectoral appendicular muscle anatomy in fossil fish and tetrapods over the fins-to-limbs transition. *Biological Reviews* 93:1077–1107. DOI: 10.1111/brv.12386.

## Question

How did the pectoral appendicular muscle complement transform across the sarcopterygian-to-tetrapod transition (~385 Mya)? Which muscles were already present in sarcopterygian fish, which split/arose at tetrapodomorph / stem-tetrapod / crown-tetrapod nodes, and which soft-tissue changes remain cryptic because they leave no osteological correlate?

Direct quote (Abstract): "*While palaeontologists, developmental biologists, and geneticists have made great strides in explaining the origin and early evolution of limb skeletal structures, that of the muscles remains largely unknown. The main reason is the lack of consensus about appendicular muscle homology between the closest living relatives of early tetrapods: lobe-finned fish and crown tetrapods.*"

## Taxa and material

**Extant bracket (Table 1)**: *Latimeria chalumnae* (Coelacanthiformes, EKUT CCC162.11 + CCC161); *Neoceratodus forsteri* (Dipnoi, MQU JVM-I-1051/1052NC); *Salamandra salamandra* (Lissamphibia, RVC JRH-SAL1–SAL5); *Ambystoma mexicanum* (HU AM1, AM2); *Sphenodon punctatus* (BMNH 1969.2204, 1935.12.6.1, UMZC R2604); *Iguana iguana* (three unnumbered specimens); plus literature re-analysis of lizards + salamanders. **Fossil ingroup (29 extinct taxa) + 6 extant**: onychodontids, dipnomorphs, tetrapodomorph fish (*Glyptolepis*, *Gogonasus*, *Sauripterus*, *Eusthenopteron*, *Rhizodopsis*, *Panderichthys*, *Tiktaalik*, etc.), stem tetrapods (*Acanthostega*, *Ichthyostega*, *Tulerpeton*, *Hynerpeton*, *Pederpes*, *Ossinodus*, *Whatcheeria*, *Greererpeton*, *Crassigyrinus*, *Baphetes*), temnospondyl *Eryops*, embolomere *Archeria*, captorhinids. Direct specimens dissected by J.L.M., R.D., J.R.H., and/or S.E.P.

## Time frame

Devonian (~385 Mya tetrapodomorph fish) → Early Carboniferous (stem tetrapods) → Early Permian (*Eryops*) → Extant (coelacanth, lungfish, salamanders, lizards, rhynchocephalians).

## Methods

Extant Phylogenetic Bracket (Witmer 1995) with extended taxon-sampling: osteological correlates of muscle attachment compiled from (a) literature and (b) direct dissection of extant taxa from the EPB. Character states for each correlate coded across 29 extinct + 6 extant sarcopterygians. Parsimony-based character optimization in **Mesquite** (Maddison & Maddison 2017) on two alternative phylogenies: Ruta, Jeffery & Coates 2003/Ruta 2011 (primary) and Ahlberg et al. 2008 (sensitivity test). Maximum likelihood methods also employed. Three supporting information character matrices (pectoral girdle S1, humerus S2, radius/ulna S3) document the scoring. Muscle-homology hypotheses from Diogo et al. 2016 as the starting framework — their one-to-one homology set from *Neoceratodus*, *Latimeria*, *Ambystoma* is the comparative input.

## Findings

1. **Muscle differentiation sequence along the sarcopterygian stem (Fig. 12, the paper's headline synthesis)**. Five nodes assigned character states:
   - **Node 1 (LCA Sarcopterygii)**: single large superficial dorsal + superficial ventral muscle mass + deep dorsal + deep ventral mass, divided by tendinous intersections into proximal + middle + distal portions. Seven muscles reconstructed (proximal/middle/distal divisions of adductor superficialis + abductor superficialis + adductor profundus + abductor profundus).
   - **Node 2 (LCA Tetrapodomorpha)**: ventral process of radius appears → separation of radial vs. ulnar/distal fin flexors → eight additional muscles reconstructed beyond sarcopterygian set.
   - **Node 3 (LCA Elpistostegalia, including *Tiktaalik*)**: **latissimus dorsi process** appears on the humerus — separation of **latissimus dorsi and deltoideus**; a scar or facet distal to the supinator ridge appears — separation of **extensor carpi radialis and brachioradialis**; first osteological correlate of **subcoracoscapularis** appears (triangular region posterior + dorsal to glenoid).
   - **Node 4 (LCA Tetrapoda)**: distinct **pectoralis process + coracobrachialis fossa** on the humerus — separation of **coracobrachialis and pectoralis**; **olecranon process + posterior ulnar crest** appear — separation of **triceps and extensor antebrachii et carpi ulnaris**.
   - **Node 5 (LCA crown Tetrapoda)**: **ventral median ridge of the radius** appears — separation of **humeroantebrachialis and flexor antebrachii et carpi radialis**.

2. **Many "tetrapod shoulder muscles" were already present in tetrapodomorph fish.** Quote (Abstract): "*Our synthesis revealed that many tetrapod shoulder muscles probably were already present in tetrapodomorph fish, while most of the more-distal appendicular muscles either arose later from largely undifferentiated dorsal and ventral muscle masses or did not leave clear correlates of attachment in these taxa.*" This is the paper's *main punchline*: the pectoral shoulder is conservative; the forearm/autopod muscles are the novel stuff that comes online at and after Tetrapoda.

3. **Superficial vs. deep muscle layer retention**. Fossil evidence is congruent with **persistence of both superficial and deep muscle layers throughout sarcopterygian history** (as Diogo et al. 2016 postulated from extant data). The first two proximodistal segments of the **superficial** mass (divided by tendinous intersections) are probably homologous with the superficial shoulder + superficial forearm musculature of tetrapods; the distal segments were either lost with lepidotrichia or incorporated into intrinsic autopodial musculature.

4. **No compelling fossil evidence for pre- and post-axial muscles or pronator/supinator muscles** in the tetrapod lineage (contra Diogo et al. 2016's predictions based on extant *Latimeria*). Preaxial/postaxial humerus scars absent in the tetrapodomorph fish surveyed except the entepicondyle. *Pronator 1* and *supinator 1* (hypothesized by Diogo et al. 2016 to be homologous with subcoracoscapularis + coracoradialis respectively) may have persisted, but may equally have been lost in the tetrapod lineage.

5. **Ventral humerus processes = sarcopterygian synapomorphy.** The paper supports the Ahlberg 1989 hypothesis that dorsal + ventral processes of the humerus are a sarcopterygian synapomorphy, homologous with the entepicondyle + ectepicondyle of tetrapods. In early tetrapodomorphs (e.g., rhizodontids): dorsal process → ectepicondyle + deltoid/supinator ridge; ventral process → oblique ventral ridge + entepicondyle. Later tetrapodomorph fish + stem tetrapods: dorsal + supinator processes become separate; latissimus dorsi process + scapulohumeral depression emerge; pectoralis process separates from the oblique ventral ridge.

6. **Osteological correlate tables (Tables 4–6)** are the practical deliverable — 11 pectoral-girdle characters, 20 humeral characters, 20 radius/ulna characters, each with column-by-column ancestor reconstructions at LCA Sarcopterygii / Tetrapodomorpha / Tetrapoda / crown Tetrapoda. These are **directly useable by anyone reconstructing a fossil limb** — e.g., "ventral process of humerus = pectoralis insertion in tetrapods," "latissimus dorsi process on posterior humerus = separation of LD from deltoid at Elpistostegalia."

7. **Confidence gradient for muscle reconstructions** in extinct taxa (Conclusions §3):
   - *Eusthenopteron* (tetrapodomorph): **high-confidence** for deltoideus, pectoralis, forearm flexors + extensors, procoracohumeralis, supracoracoideus (via Diogo et al. 2016 homology).
   - *Ossinodus* (stem tetrapod): **high-confidence** for deltoideus, pectoralis, forearm flexors + extensors, procoracohumeralis, supracoracoideus (Bishop 2014) + latissimus dorsi, subcoracoscapularis, triceps, coracobrachialis, humeroantebrachialis, extensor antebrachii et carpi ulnaris, flexor antebrachii et carpi radialis.
   - Reconstructions showing tetrapod-like muscles in **tetrapodomorph fish** like *Eusthenopteron* "*should be regarded as highly speculative*"; in **stem tetrapods** (like *Ossinodus*) "*supported by both fossil and comparative data*."

8. **Homology table (Table 2, from Diogo et al. 2016)** maps every pectoral muscle of *Latimeria* / *Neoceratodus* / *Salamandra* / *Iguana* onto four muscle groups (superficial + deep dorsomesial; superficial + deep ventrolateral). *Latimeria*'s 19 pectoral muscles map onto the tetrapod complement; *Neoceratodus*'s 4 pectoral muscles represent **secondary simplification** (reduced from ancestral sarcopterygian state), not plesiomorphy. This corrects the common "lungfish-as-proxy-for-ancestral-tetrapod" use of *Neoceratodus* in the teaching literature.

## Limitations

(1) **Salamander girdle is entirely cartilaginous except for the scapula**, severely limiting osteological-correlate tracing backward into the stem tetrapod lineage. Only a few muscles leave visible girdle scars in salamanders (1/10 in *Salamandra*) or even lizards (2/10 in *Iguana*). (2) **Pre-/post-axial and pronator/supinator muscles have no identifiable fossil correlates** — the paper can neither confirm nor refute Diogo et al. 2016's prediction of their persistence through the tetrapod lineage. (3) **Many muscles had to be scored "uncertain"** across multiple nodes in Tables 4–6 (see especially Table 6, radius/ulna). (4) **Phylogenetic sensitivity**: the Ahlberg et al. 2008 alternative phylogeny delays the appearance of the latissimus-dorsi process + coracobrachialis fossa to the *Ichthyostega* + crownward node (rather than at Tetrapoda), and delays the subcoracoscapularis correlate to the *Acanthostega*-onward node. Choice of phylogeny shifts character-state timing. (5) Reconstructions in **tetrapodomorph fish** (e.g., *Eusthenopteron*) are explicitly flagged as **highly speculative**; those in **stem tetrapods** (*Ossinodus*) as well-supported. (6) The *Neoceratodus* 4-muscle pectoral system is **secondarily reduced**, so lungfish is a misleading proxy for "ancestral tetrapod muscle condition" — a common teaching trap this paper flags.

## Relevance to comparative anatomy teaching

- **Definitive teaching reference** for the fins-to-limbs muscle transition. **Fig. 12** is the one-slide "sequence of muscle differentiation" image. Use it as the soft-tissue companion to the standard Shubin/Daeschler skeleton-centric *Tiktaalik* → *Acanthostega* → *Ichthyostega* sequence.
- **Correct the "lungfish as the ancestral condition" fallacy**: *Neoceratodus*'s 4-muscle pectoral system is derived simplification, not retained plesiomorphy. Pair with Diogo & Abdala 2007 (osteichthyan pectoral plesiomorphy = 2 muscles — but from actinopterygian perspective) and Diogo, Abdala, Aziz, Lonergan & Wood 2009 (sarcopterygian pectoral part 2).
- **Two deep messages**:
  1. Pectoral shoulder musculature (pectoralis, deltoideus, latissimus, supracoracoideus, triceps) is **already in place in tetrapodomorph fish** — the shoulder is conservative.
  2. Most distal forearm/autopodial muscle diversity is a **crown-Tetrapoda novelty**, emerging from breakdown of the single superficial ventral + superficial dorsal mass.
- **Soft-tissue EPB methodology**: Witmer 1995 Level I inferences strict-sense vs. Level II (soft tissue in extant sister group but no osteological correlate in fossil). Molnar et al. rely only on Level I + II.
- **Pair with**: Boisvert 2009 (*Panderichthys* pectoral fin); Shubin, Daeschler & Jenkins 2006 (*Tiktaalik roseae*); Pierce, Clack & Hutchinson 2012 (*Ichthyostega* 3D limb mobility); Diogo et al. 2016 (*Latimeria* fin muscles + homology hypotheses); Allen et al. 2014 (crocodilian limb architecture); Sigurdsen & Bolt 2009 (lissamphibian humerus characters). Fig. 12 combined with the Sigurdsen & Bolt elbow-reform paper gives a two-figure story for the Paleozoic-to-modern-amphibian forelimb module in lecture.

## What was mined from it (2026-08-11)

Source key: `molnar-etal-2018`. **Tetrapodomorpha (stem) went 0% → 79%**, from 7
occurrences on one species to 42 across four: *Eusthenopteron foordi*, *Tiktaalik
roseae*, *Acanthostega gunnari* and *Ossinodus pueri* (added to
`data/species.json` in this pass).

**Not from the supplementary tables.** `MINING.md` had named Tables S1–S6 as the
only route into this column. They are not in the PDF, and Tables 2 and 4–6 — the
homology table and the three character mappings — are **images**: `pdftotext`
gives their captions and nothing under them. The route is §III, "Review of
osteological correlates in fossil sarcopterygians", which walks the girdle,
humerus and radius/ulna taxon by taxon, read against §IV's proposed homologies,
§V's node reconstructions and the confidence gradient in the paragraph before the
Conclusions.

**The confidence gradient is the mining instrument.** For *Eusthenopteron*, the
highest-confidence muscles are the deltoideus, pectoralis and the forearm flexor
and extensor **masses**; the procoracohumeralis and supracoracoideus follow on
homology grounds. But: "*The subcoracoscapularis, coracobrachialis longus and
brevis, latissimus dorsi, triceps, and other individual forearm muscles were
reconstructed by Andrews & Westoll (1970a), but we did not find compelling
evidence that they existed as separate muscles in early tetrapodomorph fish.*"

That sentence contradicts what the dataset already had. Four of the seven existing
*Eusthenopteron* rows cited this paper for muscles it declines to support, and two
of their notes were wrong on its own evidence:

- the triceps note read "inferred from the olecranon" — tetrapodomorph-fish ulnae
  **lack** an ossified olecranon; it and the posterior ulnar crest appear at
  Tetrapoda, and their appearance is what marks the triceps separating from the
  extensor antebrachii et carpi ulnaris;
- the latissimus dorsi note claimed a dorsal humeral scar as its insertion — the
  latissimus dorsi process is **absent** in *Eusthenopteron* and first appears at
  Elpistostegalia.

Those four are now `uncertain`, with the attributions kept and attributed to
Andrews & Westoll. Six `uncertain` rows in total, including *Tiktaalik*'s
pectoralis (no pectoralis process) and subcoracoscapularis (the node
reconstruction puts a correlate here; the specimen shows only generic deep dorsal
rugosities, and the alternative phylogeny delays it).

**Ten correlates added to `skeleton.json`**: `ventral-cleithral-ridge`,
`ventral-humeral-ridge`, `latissimus-dorsi-process`, `supinator-process`,
`scapulohumeral-depression`, the four Bishop ridges of the radius
(`ventral-median-ridge-radius`, `dorsomesial-ridge-radius`,
`proximoventral-ridge-radius`, `ventral-radial-crest`) and
`posterior-ulnar-crest`. The interclavicle joined the stem column's presence list,
which is what lets the *Ossinodus* pectoralis origin validate.

**One internal tension recorded rather than resolved.** The ventral median ridge
of the radius is listed among the four ridges of the *Acanthostega* radius and
labelled in Fig. 10F, yet the character mapping puts its first appearance at the
LCA of crown tetrapods, where it marks the humeroantebrachialis separating from
the flexor antebrachii et carpi radialis. The rows follow the description of the
bone and say so.

**Still to take:** *Pederpes finneyae* (lateral fossa and posterior triangular
region on the scapula, spike-like latissimus process, four radial ridges),
*Ichthyostega* (pectoralis process separated by the prepectoral space) and
*Panderichthys*. All are described in the same section.

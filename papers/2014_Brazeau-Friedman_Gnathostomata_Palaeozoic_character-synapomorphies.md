# Brazeau & Friedman 2014 — The characters of Palaeozoic jawed vertebrates

## Citation

Brazeau MD, Friedman M. 2014. The characters of Palaeozoic jawed vertebrates. *Zoological Journal of the Linnean Society* 170: 779–821. doi:10.1111/zoj.12111. Open access CC-BY-NC-ND.

## Question

What, if anything, are Placodermi and Acanthodii as clades? The authors' framing: "character lists, designed to support the monophyly of putative groups, tend to overstate their strength and lack cladistic corroboration. By contrast, synapomorphy hierarchies are more open to refutation and must explicitly confront conflicting evidence." Three declared questions: (1) why has palaeontological research failed to populate the naked stem branches of the chondrichthyan, osteichthyan, and crownward gnathostome lineages (Fig. 1)? (2) What are the principal characters describing the hierarchy of these three gnathostome branches incident to the gnathostome crown? (3) What are the implications of the resulting scheme for interpreting problematic or unusual taxa? The opening epigraph from Denison 1979: "the evidence from all useful characters must be weighed impartially, and unbiased comparisons must be made with all potentially related groups."

## Taxa and material

Palaeozoic gnathostomes, broadly. Explicit focus on: **Placodermi** (arthrodires, petalichthyids, ptyctodonts, antiarchs, rhenanids, including *Macropetalichthys*, *Brindabellaspis*, *Dicksonosteus*, *Buchanosteus confertituberculatus*, *Kujdanowiaspis*, *Romundina*, *Entelognathus*, *Torosteus pulchellus*); **Acanthodii** (*Acanthodes*, *Ischnacanthus gracilis*, *Climatius*, *Parexus*, *Ptomacanthus*, *Promesacanthus eppleri*, *Brochoadmones milesi*, *Kathemacanthus*, *Seretolepis*, *Homalacanthus*, *Cheiracanthus*, *Mesacanthus*, *Paucicanthus*, *Tetanopsyrus*); **stem chondrichthyans and osteichthyans** (*Cladoselache*, *Cladodoides*, *Tristychius arcuatus*, *Helodus simplex*, *Doliodus problematicus*, *Pucapampella*, *Gladbachus*, *Ptomacanthus*, *Mimipiscis*, *Griphognathus whitei*, *Gogonasus*, *Onychodus jandemarrai*, *Lawrenciella*); **jawless outgroups** (osteostracans *Norselaspis*, *Benneviaspis*, *'Cephalaspis' powriei*, *Hemicyclaspis murchisoni*, *Superciliaspis*; heterostracan *Errivaspis waynensis*; anaspid *Birkenia*; galeaspids; pituriaspids; thelodonts). Materials are catalogued museum specimens at NHMUK (Natural History Museum, UK), NMS (National Museums of Scotland), and UALVP (University of Alberta Laboratory of Vertebrate Paleontology).

## Time frame

Palaeozoic — explicitly **Silurian through Devonian** for the focal fossil evidence, with reference to Ordovician arandaspids (*Sacabambaspis*) and Carboniferous chondrichthyans (*Akmonistion*, *Tristychius arcuatus*). Cladistic framework rooted at extant lineages (Fig. 1: Cyclostomata outgroup; Chondrichthyes + Osteichthyes as reciprocally monophyletic crown clades).

## Methods

**Character-hierarchy synthesis, not a tree-search cladistic analysis.** The paper explicitly "derives a verbal character list without an associated tree search" (p. 10). Three-step methodology:

1. **Identify three common flaws** in prior early-gnathostome cladistic work: (a) self-referential assumptions of monophyly (scoring polarity within the presumed clade, no real outgroup comparison); (b) assembly of character lists without accompanying tree-based test; (c) compound characters that conflate multiple independent conditions into a single cladistic statement.
2. **Review each previously proposed synapomorphy** of Placodermi (Young 2010's list of 16) and Acanthodii (Maisey 1986's 2, Burrow & Turner 2010's 1) against those criteria. Retain only characters that pass outgroup-based polarity + non-compound + observable-across-candidate-taxa tests.
3. **Assemble a nested hierarchical synapomorphy scheme** (64 characters total) organized by phylogenetic level of attachment: gnathostome total group → crownward of 'ostracoderms' → crownward of any placoderm (crown Gnathostomata total group) → chondrichthyan total group → osteichthyan total group.

Compound-character decomposition is illustrated with Fig. 2: the Burrow & Turner 2010 "acanthodian scapulocoracoid synapomorphy" — "a perichondrally ossified scapulocoracoid, with a slender dorsal shaft widening out basally to a blade that articulates with the pectoral fin spine" — is broken into ≥5 separate states: (i) presence of scapulocoracoid (general gnathostome character); (ii) perichondral ossification (general gnathostome character); (iii) presence of a dorsal shaft (shared with chondrichthyans); (iv) ventral widening of the scapular blade (also in chondrichthyans); (v) articulation with fin spine (in placoderms + possibly spine-bearing chondrichthyans). Under acanthodian monophyly the compound character maps to a zero-length branch; under a collapsed Acanthodii, the components map to different internal nodes.

Framework phylogenetic assumptions (stated explicitly, p. 13): (1) Gnathostomata monophyletic, crown = Osteichthyes + Chondrichthyes; (2) Chondrichthyes + Osteichthyes reciprocally monophyletic and sister taxa — neither is "more basal"; (3) Osteostraci is the sister group of all mandibulate gnathostomes (paired appendages, epicercal tail, perichondral mineralization, cellular bone).

## Findings

1. **Two characters could most parsimoniously be interpreted as placoderm synapomorphies under an outgroup-based approach**: (17) perichondral division of the orbitonasal unit (rhinocapsular ossification discrete from rest of neurocranium), (18) semidentine (polarized, matrix-bound dentine cell spaces). **Both are unsatisfactory**: character 17 has been dismissed as a generalized vertebrate trait (Goujet 2001); character 18 is not universal across placoderms (Giles, Rücklin & Donoghue 2013 showed not all placoderms exhibit a superficial dentinous layer). Placoderm monophyly is therefore weakly supported at best.
2. **Young 2010's 16 "placoderm synapomorphies" mostly fail on review.** Each is re-examined; the recurring problems are: (a) character is a compound of separate states; (b) polarity is only assessable against osteichthyan outgroup (circular when Acanthodes is the problematic anchor); (c) character is a generalized gnathostome plesiomorphy (e.g., macromeric skull, jawed-vertebrate opercular suspension); (d) character is only scorable in presence of macromeric skull — selects for the character, not the clade. Example (character 1, "distinctive pattern of dermal bones in skull roof, cheek, operculum"): "This is too vaguely stated to be subject to any specific test... it is indeed the case that the cranial bones of placoderms are identified in terms of those bones in other placoderm species under the assumption of a symplesiomorphic pattern."
3. **Only one character passes the test as a potential acanthodian synapomorphy**: (28) presence of an anal-fin spine (Fig. 4D: *Promesacanthus eppleri*). Even this one is weak — *Paucicanthus* retains the anal-fin spine but lacks spines on paired fins; the anal-spine character alone cannot distinguish "all acanthodians are stem chondrichthyans + anal spine plesiomorphic for gnathostomes" from "acanthodian monophyly + anal spine as shared derived state." Burrow & Turner 2010's scapulocoracoid character is rejected as a compound (finding 2).
4. **Hierarchical synapomorphy list, by phylogenetic level** (the paper's core deliverable):
   - **Characters 1–6, gnathostome total group (crownward of 'ostracoderms')**: perichondral bone (Fig. 5A,B); endoskeletal mineralization of sclerotic capsule; epicercal caudal fin (Fig. 4C,D); anal fin (Fig. 4B–D); paired fins (Fig. 3B,C); dermal sclerotic ring.
   - **Characters 7–16, gnathostome total group crownward of osteostracans (mandibulate gnathostomes)**: dorsoventrally opposing jaws (Fig. 9); dermal denticle plates on mandibular cartilages (Fig. 9); pelvic fins; postorbital process (Figs. 7C–E, 8); deep branching of N.VII (Fig. 7C–E) — facial nerve divides into palatine + hyomandibular branches deep to postorbital process; articulation of hyoid arch with neurocranium posterior to orbit (Fig. 6); utricular recess; horizontal semicircular canal; uninvested jugular canal lateral to otic capsule (Fig. 7C–E); five or fewer branchial arches.
   - **Characters 17–18**, tentative placoderm synapomorphies (both problematic — see finding 1).
   - **Characters 19–27, crown-gnathostome total-group synapomorphies (crownward of any placoderm)**: extrinsic eye muscle innervated by N.IV originates anterodorsal to optic foramen; otico-occipital fissure (Figs. 6D,E, 8); posterior dorsal fontanelle (Fig. 8B–D); ventral cranial fissure (Fig. 9); thin endocranial wall; sinus superior; labyrinth cavity confluent with remainder of endocranial chamber (capsular wall absent); extended telencephalic region (or correspondingly elongate sphenoid); teeth (possibly homoplasious in arthrodires; Fig. 10).
   - **Character 28**, acanthodian synapomorphy: anal-fin spine (Fig. 4D).
   - **Characters 29–39, chondrichthyan total-group synapomorphies**: precerebral fontanelle (Fig. 8C,D); canals for lateral dorsal aortae in basioccipital; dorsal otic ridge (Fig. 8C,D); tessellate prismatic calcified cartilage (Fig. 5C,D); micromeric skull; areally growing scales; tooth files with unfused bases (Fig. 10C); denticles on fin side of dorsal-fin spines (Fig. 11); pelvic claspers; dorsal scapular blade; lateral sensory line passing between flank scales. Most have superscript-A flags indicating placement contingent on *Acanthodes* position (on chondrichthyan vs. osteichthyan stem).
   - **Characters 40–64, osteichthyan total-group synapomorphies (refined from Friedman & Brazeau 2010)**: ≤2 spino-occipital nerve openings; endocranial cavity dorsally restricted within sphenoid (tropibasy component); mediolaterally sloping otic-capsule ventral surface; spiracular grooves on basisphenoid; vestibular fontanelles; horizontal semicircular canal joins posterior ampulla (Davis et al. 2012 supplement); absence of endolymphatic-duct openings in dermal skull; anterior dorsal fontanelle (Fig. 8B); enamel; endochondral bone (Fig. 5B); hyomandibular articulates with lateral commissure/postorbital process; hyomandibular branch of VII exits into jugular canal; long canals for olfactory tracts (greatly elongated in sarcopterygians; Fig. 5B); ethmoid comineralized with sphenoid, fully encloses nasal capsules; hypohyal linking ceratohyal and basihyal; first two gill arches articulate with common basibranchial; suprapharyngobranchials; anteriorly directed infrapharyngobranchials; hyomandibular bears deep groove/canal for hyomandibular N.VII branch; biconcave glenoid on lower jaw; macromeric skull; pectoral girdle with substantial dermal component; lepidotrichia; proximally restricted radials supporting hypochordal caudal lobe; dermal bones lining palate and lower jaw (entopterygoid, prearticular).
5. **Placoderms interpreted as a paraphyletic array of stem gnathostomes** (Fig. 13). Order on the gnathostome stem: Osteostraci → *Brindabellaspis* → *Macropetalichthys* → Arthrodira → *Entelognathus* → crown Gnathostomata. "We interpret placoderms as a paraphyletic array of stem-group gnathostomes." Antiarchs are omitted as a "wildcard" — their pelvic-fin absence is most likely derived (Zhu et al. 2012b) but the group's other affinities remain poorly constrained.
6. **Two equally plausible placements for acanthodians** (Fig. 13A,B): (A) acanthodian genera distributed on both chondrichthyan + osteichthyan stems — *Acanthodes* + close relatives on osteichthyan stem; (B) all acanthodians on chondrichthyan stem. The two alternatives differ by only two steps under their scheme — "we regard this as insufficient to distinguish between them at present." Resolution depends on resolving three problems: whether *Kathemacanthus* + *Seretolepis* are monophyletic or paraphyletic (Fig. 12), whether areal scale growth is an acanthodian plesiomorphy or chondrichthyan synapomorphy, and whether *Acanthodes* is an osteichthyan or chondrichthyan.
7. ***Entelognathus* sits between arthrodires and crown Gnathostomata** (Zhu et al. 2013, accepted). It is "an arthrodire-like fish that exhibits marginal jaw bones and a facial jaw and gular skeleton, previously unreported in any placoderm" — provides corroboration for a crownward placement of arthrodire-like taxa but simultaneously implies that all acanthodians are total-group chondrichthyans.
8. **Compound characters are the main source of artefactual support for Placodermi and Acanthodii.** "Morphologists understandably seek to give greater precision to their characters by composing multiple conditions to identify them. Unfortunately, this fails to distinguish compatibility from congruence, and leaves aside the possibility that the compounded conditions may individually have greater levels of phylogenetic generality." Decomposition — as in Fig. 2 — routinely collapses a proposed synapomorphy.
9. **Self-referential assumptions of monophyly infect standard datasets.** Goujet & Young 1995's 49-character matrix assumed an all-zero hypothetical ancestor with at least 32 of the "primitive" states observed only within other placoderm taxa — "they had no relevant comparator in any non-placoderms." The assumed primitive characters of placoderms are thus largely based on the presupposition of monophyly and hypotheses about which placoderms can be viewed as primitive. This re-occurs uncritically in Goujet 2001, Smith & Johanson 2003, Goujet & Young 2004, Johanson & Smith 2005, Carr, Lelièvre & Jackson 2010.
10. **Osteostracans are the sister group of mandibulate gnathostomes**, not thelodonts or anaspids — supported by four characters shared with jawed vertebrates: paired pectoral appendages (Fig. 3), epicercal tail (Fig. 4), perichondral mineralization, cellular bone (Donoghue, Sansom & Downs 2006). Thelodonts are rejected as sister to gnathostomes because thelodont scales lie somewhere on the gnathostome stem but not adjacent to it; anaspid paired fins are convergent with gnathostome paired fins (Blom & Märss 2010; Gess, Coates & Rubidge 2006). Galeaspids + pituriaspids retain paired nasal openings + buccohypophyseal features that make them more gnathostome-like than osteostracans in some characters, but the authors are conservative and retain osteostracans as sister.
11. **Ventral cranial fissure** (character 22; Fig. 9) separating basisphenoid from basioccipital was long considered an *Acanthodes* + osteichthyan synapomorphy, but the fissure is now known in stem chondrichthyans *Pucampella* and an unnamed form from South Africa (Maisey & Anderson 2001). The character is retained as a general crown-gnathostome synapomorphy with recognized ambiguity: either gain + independent loss in crown chondrichthyans, or independent acquisition in *Pucampella* + Osteichthyes.
12. **Dental homology hierarchy** (characters 9, 27): dermal tooth plates on mandibular cartilages are a mandibulate-gnathostome character; true teeth (with whatever developmental definition is applied) are a crown-gnathostome synapomorphy, potentially homoplasious in arthrodires. Arthrodire tooth tubercles "are *kinds of dermal tubercle* borne on the biting surfaces of the jaws" — sufficient for primary homology with crown-gnathostome teeth as dental structures, regardless of whether placoderms are a clade or a grade.
13. **Historical retrospective**: Hussakoff 1906 already recognized that placoderms "united on negative evidence... rather than for the possession of a series of common characters" and Moy-Thomas 1939: "the more knowledge of them [placoderms] has increased the more certain it has become, that they represent a large early gnathostome group, probably containing the ancestors of all modern fishes." The paraphyletic interpretation is a return to Moy-Thomas, not a new claim.

## Key figures

- **Fig. 1.** Assumed phylogenetic framework — Cyclostomata as outgroup; Chondrichthyes (Elasmobranchii + Holocephali) + Osteichthyes (Sarcopterygii + Actinopterygii) as reciprocally monophyletic sister groups. The ground truth on which all character polarity in the paper hangs.
- **Fig. 2.** Compound-character decomposition — Burrow & Turner 2010 acanthodian scapulocoracoid synapomorphy breaks into 5 sub-states mapping to different nodes; "zero-length branch" problem illustrated. The methodological money figure.

Shows the rearward migration of the hyoid articulation relative to the orbit across the stem and the N.II foramen position.
- **Fig. 7.** Endocranial cavities: *Benneviaspis* (osteostracan), *Brindabellaspis*, *Kujdanowiaspis*, *Buchanosteus* (placoderms), *Cladodoides* (chondrichthyan). Shows the invested-vs-uninvested jugular vein + deep branching of N.VII — major crown-gnathostome synapomorphy complex.

- **Fig. 10.** Gnathostome dental anatomy: *Torosteus pulchellus* (arthrodire), *Ischnacanthus gracilis* (acanthodian), *Cladoselache* (chondrichthyan tooth whorls), *Onychodus jandemarrai* (osteichthyan tooth whorls). Teeth as dermal tubercles; whorl-like cusp files as a crown-gnathostome feature.
- **Fig. 11.** Dorsal-fin spines with trailing-edge denticles: *Brochoadmones milesi* (acanthodian) + *Tristychius arcuatus* (chondrichthyan). Character 36.
- **Fig. 12.** Alternative phylogenetic placements for *Kathemacanthus* + *Seretolepis*: six topologies at 3–5 steps depending on areal-scale-growth + anal-fin-spine polarity decisions. Shows how narrowly the problem sits.
- **Fig. 13.** Summary cladograms (the one-slide-version of the paper's conclusion): (A) Osteostraci → *Brindabellaspis* → *Macropetalichthys* → Arthrodira → *Entelognathus* → Chondrichthyes + *Kathemacanthus*+*Seretolepis* + *Climatius*+*Parexus* + Ischnacanthiformes + Acanthodiformes + Osteichthyes; (B) same but with all acanthodians collapsed onto the chondrichthyan stem. **The two cladograms differ by only two steps — the paper's thesis is that the data cannot currently distinguish between them.**

## Limitations

- **No tree search, no parsimony matrix run.** Verbal character list only. The hierarchy is proposed as a framework for future analyses rather than as a direct phylogenetic result.
- **Placoderm paraphyly is a preferred interpretation, not a recovered topology.** Authors are explicit: "Although we currently favour a paraphyletic arrangement for placoderms, we do not contend that this is without problems." Recent pectinate cladograms of Brazeau 2009, Davis et al. 2012, and Zhu et al. 2013 all agree that ptyctodonts + arthrodires do not form a clade, but disagree on which is closer to the gnathostome crown.
- **Acanthodian placement is left ambiguous** between two topologies separated by two steps (Fig. 13A,B). Resolution requires new fossil material — specifically: articulated Ordovician/Silurian stem-chondrichthyan endoskeletal material to constrain the character polarity of the chondrichthyan total group.
- **Antiarchs omitted** from the primary cladogram as "wildcard" taxa — their pelvic-fin absence may be secondarily derived (Zhu et al. 2012b *Parayunnanolepis*), so their placement as sister group of all other mandibulate gnathostomes is uncertain. Most cladistic analyses place them there; the authors do not contest this but do not incorporate it into their scheme.
- **Character 31 (dorsal otic ridge)** has been identified in *Acanthodes* by Davis et al. 2012. Under the Brazeau 2009 and Davis et al. 2012 placement of *Acanthodes* on the osteichthyan stem, this creates an internal contradiction — the character is now evidence against the osteichthyan placement. The paper acknowledges this and flags it as a character to track.
- **Thelodonts, galeaspids, pituriaspids** are given a fair hearing but ultimately sidelined. The osteostracan sister-group assumption is admitted to be one of several reasonable alternatives, not the uniquely best-supported one.
- **Pituriaspid interpretation rests on natural moulds only** — "no precise details of their hard tissues can be considered." Anatomical inference from moulds is noted as uncertain.
- **No new specimens described or figured in detail** — this is a review and synthesis, not a primary descriptive paper. It is a companion to Friedman & Brazeau 2010 (osteichthyan characters) rather than a standalone data source.
- **Does not cover Silurian galeaspids like *Shuyu***; later work (Gai et al. 2022) on the Shuyu spiracle will recontextualize some of the pharyngeal-arch reconstructions not covered here.

## Relevance to comparative anatomy teaching

The most important **phylogenetic-methods** paper in the early-gnathostome corner of the library. Multiple teaching uses.

- **Fig. 13 is the headline teaching figure** — a clean one-slide summary of the current (as of 2014) early-gnathostome tree, with placoderms as paraphyletic and acanthodians as grade. Use to replace textbook phylogenies that still treat Placodermi + Acanthodii as clades. Pair with Castiello & Brazeau 2018 (petalichthyid braincase µCT corroboration) and Gai et al. 2022 (galeaspid spiracle aphetohyoidean) for a stem-gnathostome anatomy update lecture.
- **Fig. 2 is the cleanest teaching example of compound-character decomposition.** Use to show students why cladistic character coding is a non-trivial intellectual step — "counting similarity" is not the same as "counting synapomorphies." The zero-length branch issue + character mis-compounding is a common failure mode in morphology student projects.
- **The hierarchical synapomorphy list (characters 1–64) is a dissection-anatomy lecture outline**. Can be mapped onto the actual lab specimens (lamprey for outgroup — characters 1–6 absent; osteostracan fossil image — characters 1–6 present, 7–16 absent; *Dicksonosteus* or arthrodire model — characters 1–16 present; crown fish — characters 1–27+ present). Teaches students to trace character acquisition across a stem lineage rather than memorize a checklist.
- **Methods-critique teaching**. The three-flaw framework (self-referential monophyly, list-building, compound characters) applies outside early gnathostome systematics — students can be asked to apply it to any prior cladistic paper in the library (e.g., Anderson 2008 gnathostome cranial muscles, Diogo & Ziermann 2015 paired-appendage muscles, Springer & Johnson 2015 anguilliform gill-arch muscles) and discuss whether the same issues apply.
- **Pair as teaching triad with**: Brazeau 2009 *Nature* (acanthodian-osteichthyan placement of *Acanthodes*), Zhu et al. 2013 *Nature* (*Entelognathus* stem-gnathostome fish with osteichthyan-like jaw), Davis, Finarelli & Coates 2012 *Nature* (*Acanthodes* braincase as stem osteichthyan). All three are the primary data papers; Brazeau & Friedman 2014 is the character-hierarchy synthesis that frames them.
- **Pair with Giles et al. 2017** (scanilepiform stem polypterids, actinopterygian crown younger than molecular estimates) + **Ahlberg 2018** (fish-tetrapod transition review) for a "phylogenetics of the early gnathostome + early sarcopterygian radiation" lecture arc.
- **Pair with Castiello & Brazeau 2018** (petalichthyid braincase) — directly relevant: Brazeau & Friedman 2014 identifies the petalichthyid + osteostracan endocranial characters (Fig. 6: hyoid articulation lateral/anterior to orbit) that Castiello & Brazeau then quantifies via µCT.
- **Caveat for students**: the paper is long (35 pages, 64 characters) and dense. The character list is the reference data; Fig. 13 + Fig. 2 + the Introduction are the one-lecture-readable sections. Direct students to those rather than the full review.
- **Open question flagged**: the ventral cranial fissure (char. 22) is a key character whose polarity hangs on *Acanthodes* placement — useful as a "where is the data" discussion prompt.

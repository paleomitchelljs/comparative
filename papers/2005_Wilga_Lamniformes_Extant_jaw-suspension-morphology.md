# Wilga (2005) — Jaw suspension in lamniform sharks

*Journal of Morphology*. doi:10.1002/jmor.10342

## What the abstract states

- The preorbitalis is the primary muscle that protrudes the upper jaw in
  squalean sharks (the group *Squalus* belongs to) and in carcharhiniform sharks.
- It is described as running from the quadratomandibularis to the nasal capsule
  in those groups. Huber et al. (2011) label the same two ends the other way
  round (origin nasal capsule, insertion on the adductor). Both name the same two
  ends; the muscle lies along the adductor, which fits the preorbitalis being
  continuous with it.
- Carcharhiniforms have a second, dorsal preorbitalis division. Lamnids keep both
  divisions but move both insertions onto connective tissue of the upper jaw.
- The levator hyomandibularis retracts the jaws, and in derived lamniforms inserts
  more directly on the jaws.

## What it does not say

Nothing in the abstract on the timing of the muscle in the gape cycle. For that,
see Wilga et al. (2001), which reviews the *Squalus* recordings.

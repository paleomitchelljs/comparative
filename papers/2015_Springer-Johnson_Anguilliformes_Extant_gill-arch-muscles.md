# Springer & Johnson (2015) — Gill-arch musculature of *Protanguilla* and other eels

*Copeia* 103(3):595–620. doi:10.1643/CI-14-152. Source key: `springer-johnson-2015`

## Why it was reached for

Highest remaining density on `MINING.md` at 5.9 — measured **5.3**, still firmly a
descriptive myology. And `branchial-constrictors` had a hagfish, a lamprey, a shark, a
gar, an axolotl and a turtle, with nothing teleost between the gar and the tetrapods.

**Actinopterygii 46% → 50%. Cranial 57%.** One row, and it is the largest single
occurrence in the record.

## The species, and why this one

*Protanguilla palau* (USNM 396051, 156 mm TL), described in 2012 and hypothesised to
be the sister group of **all other eels, living and fossil**, on more plesiomorphic
osteology than any other eel plus mitogenomic data. The paper compares it with
*Conger cinereus*, *Anguilla rostrata*, *Synaphobranchus kaupii*, *Simenchelys
parasitica* and *Serrivomer beanii*, each described separately — five more species
available from this paper when the fish end of the record is worth widening.

Its species note says what it is not: a specialised deep-reef eel, not a proxy for
teleosts. Its interest is that it keeps characters other eels lost.

## Fifteen units, and the absences are characters too

The most divided this record is in any taxon — against ten in the axolotl, eight in
the turtle's laryngeal series, two in the shark. Present: levatores externi 1–4,
levatores interni 1–2, transversus dorsalis, MPb2-Eb1, obliquus dorsalis 4, recti
dorsales 1–4, obliquus posterior, adductores 4 and 5, MCb5-TP5, obliqui ventrales
1–2, recti 3 and 4.

**Absent and recorded as such:** obliquus dorsalis 3, adductores 1–3, recti 1 and 2,
R4Cm. The paper's purpose is phylogenetic — nine anguilliform synapomorphies, seven
myological, all but two reported for the first time — so these attachments are
characters first and anatomy second. Two are carried in the row's notes:

- **only *Protanguilla* has a rectus dorsalis 4** among elopomorphs;
- **a cartilaginous or absent hypobranchial 3 is an anguilliform synapomorphy**, that
  element being largely ossified in all other elopomorphs and most other teleosts —
  a synapomorphy Johnson et al. (2012) had not noted.

## It forced the branchial basket into the skeleton

The record's fish rows had been attaching to `branchial-arches`, a single group
element. That cannot express a **rectus dorsalis running from one epibranchial onto
the preceding one**, which is the diagnostic arrangement here. Three elements added,
all `partOf` the branchial arches and scoped to the ray-finned fishes:
`epibranchials`, `pharyngobranchials`, `hypobranchials`.

**The ceratobranchial did not get a new element.** `cornu-branchiale` already holds
that element for turtles, salamanders and frogs, so the ray-finned fishes joined its
presence list and it gained a `taxonNames` entry reading *Ceratobranchial* for them.
One element, per-taxon names — the same rule that keeps the hyomandibula and the
stapes in one record.

## One inference inside a directly observed set

RecD1's insertion **was lost when the gill arches were removed**, and the authors
infer from *Anguilla* that it attached to the dorsal tip of the posterior ceratohyal.
That is the ceratohyal row, and the `attachmentNote` says which row it is, because
everything else in the set was seen.

The levator externus origins are the other soft spot: they arise on the cranium and
Springer & Johnson state that **the exact position was not recorded**. So the origin
row is `neurocranium` with no landmark.

## Still in it

*Conger*, *Anguilla*, *Synaphobranchus*, *Simenchelys* and *Serrivomer*, each fully
described — five species that would turn this record's teleost end into a series with
real within-clade disagreement. The osteological synapomorphies, and the elopomorph
and osteoglossomorph comparisons that would reach outside Anguilliformes.

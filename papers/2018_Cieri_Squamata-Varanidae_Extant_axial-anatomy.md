# Cieri (2018) — The axial anatomy of monitor lizards (Varanidae)

*J. Anat.* 233:636–643. `cieri-2018`. Three *Varanus exanthematicus* dissected,
with rib counts surveyed across 26 varanid species.

## Why it is in the corpus

The only description of lepidosaur trunk musculature here, and axial is the
thinnest region in the dataset. It is also a non-standard animal described in its
own right rather than as a stand-in for "the lizard".

## What it gives

**The hypaxial series, muscle by muscle** — obliquus externus in two portions,
the two intercostal layers, transversus, rectus abdominis, levator costae. Each
with origin and insertion in prose.

**Two muscles varanids have that iguanas do not**: `levator costae` and
`supracostalis dorsus brevis`. Both connect a rib directly to the vertebral
column rather than to another rib, so they can move each rib independently of the
rib cage — Cieri reads the supracostalis as driving the caliper motion behind the
dorsoventral flattening monitors show in basking, combat and threat display.

**Obliquus internus is absent in varanids.** Stated plainly, and it is why this
group has one oblique layer where a mammal has two.

**Rib morphology** (his Fig. 2): cervical, true, floating and 'abdominal' ribs,
with the varanid sternal portion read as an incomplete fusion of the ancestral
intercostal and sternocostal ribs.

## The limitation that matters most

**He explicitly declines to describe the epaxial attachments.** In his own words,
the anatomy of longissimus dorsi, transversospinalis and iliocostalis, *including
details of insertion and origin tendons*, has been described previously by Gasc
(1981), Ritter (1995) and Tsuihiji (2007), and "will not be described here".

Those four rows therefore stay unscored, carrying a note that says so. **This is a
pointer rather than a gap**: Gasc, Ritter and Tsuihiji are what would close the
lepidosaur epaxial column, and none of the three is in the corpus. They are the
acquisition this pass identified.

He also does not dissect *Iguana*; the comparison rests on Carrier (1989, 1990).

## Attachment pass (August 2026)

**A row was on the wrong animal, and the row itself said so.** `levator-costae`
carried a single occurrence — *Iguana iguana*, `present: yes`, sourced to this
paper — whose own note read "Absent in *Iguana iguana*". It asserted presence and
absence simultaneously, and Cieri names this muscle as one of the two *Varanus*
has and *Iguana* lacks. Re-attributed to *V. exanthematicus* with his attachments:
dorsocaudal aspect of the diapophysis onto the dorsal edge of the cranial aspect
of the next vertebral rib segment.

**No replacement Iguana row.** Cieri did not examine *Iguana*, so an absence row
citing him would credit him with an observation he never made. The claim is in the
record's `homology.notes` as the comparative statement it is.

Also scored: the rectus abdominis (entire cranial pubis to the ventral sternum,
blending with the pectoralis), and the hypaxial group row as the union of the
body-wall muscles he describes. The `obliquus-externus` row was missing the
**profundus origin on the pubes** — with it the muscle is a pelvis-to-rib-cage
connection and not only a body-wall sheet.

Axial 50% → 57%, Lepidosauria 68% → 69%.

# Werneburg & Preuschoft (2024) — Temporal skull openings and jaw mechanics

## Citation

Werneburg I, Preuschoft H. 2024. Evolution of the temporal skull openings in land
vertebrates: a hypothetical framework on the basis of skull biomechanics. *Journal
of Experimental Zoology* B.

## The argument

A basic biomechanical account of the land vertebrate skull, concentrating on the
**cranial openings in the temporal region**. They isolate two feeding-related
factors that largely shape the skull:

1. **Where the most forceful biting is conducted**, and
2. **the handling of resisting forces** from the food.

The temporal fenestrae follow from those — the openings are where bone is not
needed, given where the load is.

## What it is used for here

Cited on **`adductor-mandibulae-externus`** as background. It is the osteological
correlate side of cranial muscle reconstruction: the fenestra bounds the adductor
chamber, and its shape carries information about the volume, orientation and loading
of the muscle that filled it.

That is a real correlate and an unusually direct one — most cranial muscles leave no
scar, and the chamber is the evidence.

**No occurrence rows**, correctly: it is a framework paper.

## Relevance to comparative anatomy teaching

The paper for the moment a student asks why synapsids, diapsids and anapsids are
named for their holes. The answer here is mechanical rather than taxonomic, which is
more useful and harder to memorise wrongly. Pair with **Werneburg (2011)** for the
turtle adductor chamber in detail and **Werneburg & Maier (2019)** for how the two
turtle clades built theirs differently.

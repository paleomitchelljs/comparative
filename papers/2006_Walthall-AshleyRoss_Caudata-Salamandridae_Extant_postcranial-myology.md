# Walthall & Ashley-Ross 2006 — Postcranial myology of the California newt, *Taricha torosa*

**Citation.** Walthall, J. C. & Ashley-Ross, M. A. 2006. Postcranial myology of the California newt, *Taricha torosa*. *The Anatomical Record Part A* 288A(1): 46–57. DOI: 10.1002/ar.a.20279. Open access (bronze) at the publisher; no PDF held locally.

## Question

Salamanders are the standard postural model for early tetrapods, yet careful muscle-by-muscle descriptions of them are scarce, and what exists is dominated by *Necturus* — a permanently aquatic form. The authors dissect a primarily terrestrial newt to provide (a) baseline anatomy for electromyographic work on newt locomotion in water and on land, and (b) a synonymy tying the forelimb muscles to the names earlier workers used for other salamander genera.

## Taxa + material

Four formalin-fixed *Taricha torosa* (Rathke, 1833), collected in Orange County, California. Dissected under a Zeiss Stemi 11V with camera lucida; iodine staining applied periodically to resolve fibre direction and connective tissue. Figures traced from camera lucida drawings.

Comparative genera drawn on throughout the discussion: *Salamandra* (Francis 1934), *Amphiuma* (Davison 1895), *Typhlomolge* (Emerson 1905), *Necturus* (Gilbert 1973), *Cryptobranchus* / "*Menopoma*" (Mivart 1869), *Bolitoglossa* (Restrepo & Hoyos 1998), *Triturus* (Smith 1927), *Ambystoma* and *Dicamptodon* (Ashley-Ross 1992, 1995), *Paramesotriton* (Darevsky & Salomatina 1989), *Pseudoeurycea* (Baird 1951), *Onychodactylus*.

## Time frame

Extant. The framing claim is that salamander anatomy has been essentially unchanged for at least 150 million years (Gao & Shubin 2001), which is what licenses using a newt as a postural analogue for Palaeozoic tetrapods.

## Methods

Straight comparative dissection with a stated origin and insertion for every muscle. Function is handled with unusual discipline: where activity has been measured by electromyography in another salamander the reference is given (almost all to Ashley-Ross 1995 on *Dicamptodon*), and where it has not, the text says "presumed function". Roughly half the muscles are presumed.

## Findings

1. **Hypaxial trunk musculature is the ordinary vertebrate arrangement** — three obliquely oriented layers under a superficial rectus abdominis. Fibre angles are measured rather than described: external oblique about 50° to the body axis, internal oblique about 145°, so the two cross at close to a right angle, and the transversus runs dorsoventrally. Only the transversus reaches the vertebral column; the obliques run fascia to fascia and leave no osteological trace at either end.

2. **The extensor iliotibialis has a single head in *Taricha*,** against the two heads (pars anterior and posterior) usual in salamanders. Presence of the split does not track habitat or phylogeny: *Cryptobranchus*, *Necturus*, *Amphiuma* and *Typhlomolge* are all exclusively aquatic yet differ; *Salamandra*, *Pseudoeurycea*, *Ambystoma*, *Dicamptodon*, *Bolitoglossa* and *Taricha* are all primarily terrestrial and also differ. What it does track is limb robustness — robust-limbed genera have two heads, slender-limbed ones (*Amphiuma*, *Typhlomolge*, *Bolitoglossa*, *Taricha*) have one. Smith (1927) reported two heads in the congener *Taricha granulosa*, so the character may vary within the genus.

3. **The ischioflexorius is incompletely separated from the puboischiotibialis.** Only its distal section is cleanly divisible; the proximal section can be seen but not mechanically separated. *Pseudoeurycea*, *Bolitoglossa*, *Paramesotriton* and *Onychodactylus* appear to share this. The character does not follow salamander phylogeny and must have been gained or lost more than once. Functionally it may not matter much: Ashley-Ross (1995) showed the proximal puboischiotibialis and proximal ischioflexorius are active over almost the same part of the stride in *Dicamptodon*, so one can stabilise the other.

4. **The femorofibularis is enlarged in *Taricha*, and trades off in size against the ischioflexorius.** *Dicamptodon* and newly metamorphosed *Ambystoma* have a large ischioflexorius with a thin femorofibularis; *Ambystoma* well past metamorphosis has the reverse; *Paramesotriton* shows the same trade-off (Darevsky & Salomatina 1989); *Bolitoglossa* has a substantial femorofibularis with an extensive fibular insertion (Restrepo & Hoyos 1998). Francis (1934) noted that branches of one nerve supply the distal ischioflexorius and the femorofibularis. The authors suggest the two are alternative solutions to the same mechanical demand — notable because they differ enormously in span, the femorofibularis crossing only the knee while the ischioflexorius runs from the pelvis to the sole.

5. **Forelimb variation across salamanders is mostly size, not position.** The procoracohumeralis ranges from robust (*Cryptobranchus*, *Pseudoeurycea*, *Bolitoglossa*) through average (*Taricha*) to very thin (*Typhlomolge*); the latissimus dorsi is large in most and has a much less extensive origin in *Cryptobranchus*. Comparison is limited because few workers have described the forearm and manus at all — Francis (1934) and Restrepo & Hoyos (1998) are the exceptions.

6. **Fore- and hindlimb musculature converge distally.** The forearm and crus, and the manus and pes, show nearly identical arrangements; dorsal and ventral musculature of the forearm are close to mirror images. Digits are served superficially by muscles from the distal end of the proximal segment, all ending in aponeuroses from which long tendons run to the distal phalanges; below them lie muscles controlling torsion of the autopod and of the zeugopod itself; deepest are the series running from the palmar or plantar aponeurosis to the phalanges, which are indistinguishable between the limbs. The hindlimb is more differentiated between dorsal and ventral than the forelimb. The authors read the antebrachium's mirror symmetry as evidence it has undergone little locomotor remodelling, and suggest the salamander forelimb may retain the primitive tetrapod pattern — with the caveat that digit V has been lost from the manus.

7. **Table 1 is a forelimb synonymy across seven genera and seven authors** (Francis 1934 on *Salamandra*, Davison 1895 on *Amphiuma*, Emerson 1905 on *Typhlomolge*, Gilbert 1973 on *Necturus*, Mivart 1869 on *Cryptobranchus*, Restrepo & Hoyos 1998 on *Bolitoglossa*, Smith 1927 on *Triturus*; Baird 1951 follows Francis and is omitted). It separates two kinds of blank — the author did not describe the muscle, versus the muscle is absent. The sharpest column is Mivart's: what Francis names as four anconaeus muscles, Mivart calls "triceps" four times over, and what four other authors call the opercularis he calls levator anguli scapulae.

## Limitations

- Four specimens, one species, one locality, all formalin-fixed. No architectural measurement — fibre architecture is described qualitatively as "simple parallel-fibered", with no fibre lengths, pennation angles or PCSA.
- Function for roughly half the muscles is presumed from geometry, and where it is not, it is transferred from *Dicamptodon* electromyography rather than measured in *Taricha*.
- Sexes, sizes and the aquatic/terrestrial state of the specimens are not reported, which matters given that the paper's own framing is about locomotion in two environments.
- The character used to argue against a habitat explanation for the iliotibialis split — limb robustness — is assessed qualitatively.
- One evident slip: the abductor et extensor digiti I of the **hindlimb** is said to originate from "the intermedium and centrale bones of the **wrist**". The muscle is figured in the pes; the tarsal elements are meant.

## How it was scored here

Caudata attachment coverage went from 9% to 75% of present occurrences on this source. Decisions worth stating:

- **The pubo-ischiac plate is scored as `pubis` plus `ischium`,** not as an element of its own. The paper itself glosses the ischium as "the posterior portion of the pubo-ischiac plate", which is the licence for splitting it. Giving the plate its own record would put the salamander's puboischiofemoralis on a different bone from every other tetrapod's.
- **The crista ventralis is the deltopectoral crest,** added as a Caudata taxon-name on that element rather than as a second element.
- **No landmark is asserted where the paper's term is ambiguous.** The femoral "crista ventralis" is scored as `femur` / ventral rather than as the fourth trochanter; the tibial "spine" as `tibia` / anterior rather than the cnemial crest; the fibular insertion of the iliofibularis as `fibula` / posterior rather than the iliofibular tubercle. In each case the correspondence is plausible and unestablished, and asserting it would manufacture a correlate.
- **Basale V is scored as a distal tarsal.** The paper's more specific term is kept in the note rather than given an element that would recur nowhere else.
- **Carpal and tarsal intermedium are separate elements,** for the same reason radiale and tibiale are.
- **The opercularis disagreement is recorded, not resolved.** This paper describes a discrete m. opercularis in *Taricha*; Abdala & Diogo (2010), following Hetherington & Tugaoen (1990), hold the urodele structure to be part of the levator scapulae. The occurrence stays at `present: "no"` and the note carries both positions.
- **Eighteen new records** were created for muscles the dataset had no row for, most of them intrinsic pedal muscles whose manual counterparts already existed. Each Caudata occurrence on a new record says so explicitly, so a salamander-only row reads as thin sampling rather than a salamander novelty.
- **Seven skeletal elements were added,** because the description names attachment sites the ontology could not resolve: the ypsiloid cartilage, the carpal and tarsal intermedium, the tarsal centrale and the distal tarsal row — the primitive tetrapod carpals and tarsals that fuse or vanish in most amniotes — and transverse processes on the trunk and caudal vertebrae, which the ontology had only for the cervicals.
- **Creating the pes records let misfiled data go home.** Three forelimb records carried attachment rows their own notes flagged as recorded from the foot, parked there because no pedal counterpart record existed. Five consensus rows on pes muscles pointed at `phalanges-manus`, copied across from the forelimb record when the pes record was created. Both are fixed, and `abductor-digiti-minimi-pes` and `contrahentium-caput-longum-pes` arrive with two taxa rather than one. A cross-limb check is cheap and worth repeating: no row in a pelvic, thigh, leg or foot muscle should name a forelimb element, or the reverse.

## Relevance to comparative anatomy teaching

The single best source for the salamander baseline, and the one that makes the "primitive tetrapod" claim concrete rather than gestural. Three teachable points it supplies that other sources here do not:

- **A muscle can be identified by its insertion alone.** The iliocaudalis runs parallel to the epaxial muscles and is distinguishable from them *only* by ending on the ilium. Where a muscle has no visible border, its attachment is what makes it a muscle.
- **Postaxial position and digit number disagree between the limbs.** The manual extensor lateralis digiti IV and the pedal abductor digiti V occupy the same margin of the autopod on differently numbered digits, because the salamander hand has lost digit V. Matching by number pairs them with nothing; matching by position pairs them with each other.
- **Two muscles of very different span can substitute for one another in size.** The femorofibularis/ischioflexorius trade-off is a caution against reading function from length.

Pair with **Abdala & Diogo 2010** (which cites this paper for the Caudata column of its homology matrix), **Diogo & Molnar 2014** for the serial correspondences, and **Sigurdsen, Green & Bishop 2012**, which takes the single procoracoid deltoid slip described here as the plesiomorphic condition from which the anuran saltatory forelimb is derived.

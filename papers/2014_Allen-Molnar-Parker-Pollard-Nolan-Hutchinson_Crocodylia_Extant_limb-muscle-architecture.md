# Allen et al. (2014) — Limb muscle architecture in Crocodylidae and Alligatoridae

## Citation

Allen V, Molnar J, Parker W, Pollard A, Nolan G, Hutchinson JR. 2014. Comparative
architectural properties of limb muscles in Crocodylidae and Alligatoridae and
their relevance to divergent use of asymmetrical gaits in extant Crocodylia.
*Journal of Anatomy* 225: 569–582.

## Question

Crocodiles bound and gallop when moving fast. Alligators, despite being
morphologically and ecologically similar, appear not to. Is there an anatomical
basis for the difference?

## Taxa and material

**40 individuals from six representative species** across Crocodylidae and
Alligatoridae. Relative muscle masses and internal architecture — fascicle lengths
and physiological cross-sectional areas — for the pectoral and pelvic limbs.

## Findings worth carrying

**Crocodylidae have significantly longer muscle fascicles**, giving an increased
working range, **particularly in the pectoral limb**, and generally **smaller
PCSAs**, giving decreased force-exerting capability, than Alligatoridae.

Their hypothesis follows: the ability to use asymmetrical gaits may be limited more
by the ability to make large, rapid limb motions — especially at the forelimb —
than by the ability to exert large forces. The constraint is excursion, not
strength.

## Scored, and what is deliberately not

5 occurrence rows on *Alligator mississippiensis* — caudofemoralis, gastrocnemius,
pectoralis, latissimus dorsi, triceps brachii.

**Attachments are deliberately not scored from this source.** It measures
architecture and gives essentially no attachment sites — around 0.1 origin/insertion
mentions per page across the whole paper. Writing attachment rows from it would mean
importing them from somewhere else under this citation, which is the failure the
base-layer rule exists to prevent.

The caudofemoralis row does carry the one osteological fact the paper hands over
plainly: the muscle is large and well developed, and inserts on a prominent fourth
trochanter.

## Limitations

- **Not a description.** See above. It is an architecture source and is listed as
  such in `WORKLIST.md`.
- **Captive animals and a broad species sample** — good for the family-level
  contrast the paper draws, weaker for any one species.

## Relevance to comparative anatomy teaching

A tidy example of two clades with the same muscles arranged the same way behaving
differently because of fascicle length and cross-sectional area — the two
properties that never appear in a dissection-based description. Pair with **Dick &
Clemente (2016)** for the same measurements in varanids and **Hudson et al. (2011)**
for mammals.

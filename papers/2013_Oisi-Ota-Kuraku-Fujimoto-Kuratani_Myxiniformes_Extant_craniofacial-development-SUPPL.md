# Oisi, Ota, Kuraku, Fujimoto & Kuratani 2013 — hagfish craniofacial development (supplementary information)

**Citation (main paper).** Oisi, Y., Ota, K. G., Kuraku, S., Fujimoto, S. & Kuratani, S. 2013. Craniofacial development of hagfishes and the evolution of vertebrates. *Nature* 493: 175–180. DOI: 10.1038/nature11794.

**What this PDF contains.** The 16-page **Supplementary Information** only, not the main article. Twelve supplementary figures + one supplementary table + supplementary references. The original source filename was `trigeminal.pdf`, taken from Supplementary Figure 8 (trigeminal nerve innervation in hagfishes and lampreys), but the document covers far more than the trigeminal system.

## Question

Are the "weird" adult craniofacial patterns of hagfishes (external nostril rostral, velum oblique, nasohypophyseal duct opening caudally into pharynx) a deep plesiomorphy that would exclude cyclostome monophyly, or are they a derived condition sitting on top of a shared cyclostome developmental Bauplan?

## Taxa + material

- **Hagfish:** *Eptatretus burgeri*, embryonic stages 25–53 (neurula → late pharyngula), plus adult. First detailed hagfish embryonic series since Dean 1899 (*Bdellostoma stouti*) — Supplementary Table 1 cross-references Dean's stages against the new *E. burgeri* staging.
- **Lamprey:** *Lethenteron japonicum* / *Lampetra japonica*, stages 22, 24, 26, 27, 50, plus adult. Used as cyclostome comparator.
- **Shark outgroup:** *Scyliorhinus torazame*, stage 26 embryo. Provides gnathostome pattern for premandibular mesoderm, hypophyseal placode, prechordal plate, palatoquadrate/Meckel's cartilage derivation of V2/V3.

## Time frame

Extant. Developmental / embryological paper.

## Methods (inferred from figure legends + supplementary references)

- **3D reconstruction** from serial histological sections of cleared + H&E stained embryos (stages 25 → 53). Panels throughout present left-column 3D surface renderings, middle-column sagittal H&E sections, right-column higher-magnification boxes.
- **In situ hybridization** probes for developmental patterning genes: *Nkx2.1* (hypothalamus), *Six3/6A*, *PitxA*, *Lhx3/4A*, *SoxB1* (nasohypophyseal plate development); *Pax1/9* (pharyngeal pouches); *Tbx1/10A* + *LjTbx1/10* (pharyngeal mesoderm, muscle anlage).
- **Adult μCT / chondrocranial imaging** for adult hagfish vs. lamprey comparison (Fig. 1 panels f, i, m).
- **3D-reconstructed cranial nerve tracing** for trigeminal (V1, V2/3A external + internal, V2/3B), facial (VII), glossopharyngeal-vagus (IX+X) — rhombomere origins mapped onto r1–r6.

## Findings (as organized by the 12 supplementary figures)

1. **Fig. S1 — hagfish and lamprey adult/larval anatomical comparison.** Establishes the five-character divergence matrix (nostril, nasohypophyseal duct orientation + ending, velum orientation, visceral organ position). Adult hagfish has oblique, open nasohypophyseal duct; adult lamprey has vertical, blind one.

2. **Fig. S2 — *E. burgeri* embryonic series (stages 25–53).** Dorsal views of cleared embryos documenting craniofacial development from mid-neurula through late pharyngula. Otocyst, pharynx, heart, somites, velum, tentacles T1–T4 emerge in recognizable order. This is the first sequential hagfish-embryo atlas since Dean 1899.

3. **Fig. S3 — oropharyngeal membrane (OPM) development.** 3D + sagittal + high-magnification panels, stages 25 → 45. **Ectoderm and endoderm are directly juxtaposed without intervening mesenchyme** at the posterior end of the stomodeum. The OPM forms the ecto/endodermal boundary at stage 28; endoderm thins; OPM degenerates by stages 40–45. This is the first rigorous identification of the ectoderm/endoderm boundary in hagfish, correcting the earlier interpretation that the oronasohypophyseal cavity (including adenohypophysis) was prechordal-plate-derived endoderm.

4. **Fig. S4 — secondary oropharyngeal membrane (SOM) + adenohypophysis origin.** At stage 28 the anterior opening of the head is bilaterally closed; by stage 30 the oronasohypophyseal opening (ono) is almost sealed by the SOM. **Previous interpretation:** the oronasohypophyseal cavity came from anterior-most endoderm. **This paper's correction:** the purely ectodermal SOM was misidentified as the ecto-endodermal OPM. The adenohypophysis is ectodermal, not endodermal, consistent with gnathostomes. This is the headline re-interpretation.

5. **Fig. S5 — hypophyseal placode and prechordal plate in shark and hagfish.** In stage 26 *Scyliorhinus torazame*, prechordal-plate-derived premandibular mesoderm and hypophyseal placode (Rathke's pouch) form in the hypothalamus region; hypophyseal placode evaginates dorsally as in other gnathostomes. In stage 35 *E. burgeri*, prechordal plate forms a dorsal protrusion with putative premandibular mesoderm posterior to the hypothalamus. Hagfish premandibular mesoderm initially develops in the future hypothalamus vicinity, **then disappears** without differentiating any structures in later development. Lamprey (Kuratani et al. 2004) behaves the same way. **Premandibular mesoderm disappearance is a shared cyclostome feature.**

6. **Fig. S6 — embryonic gene expression in *E. burgeri*.** *Nkx2.1* in hypothalamus (stages 26 + 53). *PitxA* in ectoderm rostral to OPM including posterior NHP + stomodeum (stages 28 + 40). *Lhx3/4A* in posterior NHP (stage 40) = presumptive hypophyseal placode. *Six3/6A* + *SoxB1* in the entire nasohypophyseal plate (stages 26–40). The gene-expression signature for the NHP + hypophyseal placode matches the gnathostome pattern — the developmental modules are deeply conserved.

7. **Fig. S7 — head development compared stage by stage between lampreys and hagfishes.** The two cyclostome lineages look **most similar at the mid-pharyngula stage** (lamprey stage 26, hagfish stage 45 — highlighted by blue box in the figure). Both at this stage have a single ectodermal **nasohypophyseal plate (NHP)** bordered by two ectomesenchymal processes — anterior nasal process (ANP) + posterior hypophyseal process (PHP). After this shared stage, the PHP grows differently in the two lineages: in lamprey it grows rostrally + dorsally beneath the NHP, pushing the external nostril onto the dorsal surface; in hagfish the PHP grows anteroposteriorly to form the oronasal shelf, and the nasohypophyseal duct secondarily opens caudally into the pharynx by rupture of the root of the PHP. **The mid-pharyngula phylotypic stage is shared; divergence is post-phylotypic.** This is the paper's big-picture argument for cyclostome monophyly.

8. **Fig. S8 — trigeminal nerve innervation patterns in hagfishes + lampreys** (the figure the filename `trigeminal.pdf` derives from). 3D reconstructions of V1, V2/3A (internal + external), V2/3B in adult hagfish (stage 53), lamprey larva (stage 30), and adult lamprey. Schematic summary table:

   | nerve         | hagfish target                     | lamprey larva target      | lamprey adult target       |
   |---------------|------------------------------------|---------------------------|----------------------------|
   | V1            | prenasal region                    | prenasal region           | prenasal region            |
   | V2/3A ext     | lateral tentacles T1,3,4; mid-T2   | mid upper lip             | mid oral funnel            |
   | V2/3A int     | tentacles muscles + buccal muscles | upper lip + buccal muscles| oral funnel + buccal muscles|
   | V2/3B         | velum muscles + tongue muscles     | velum + lower lip muscles | velum + tongue muscles     |

   Developmental and morphological domains are comparable between the two cyclostomes despite the adult morphology looking wildly different. **Tentacles of hagfish are innervated by the same branches that innervate the upper lip of the lamprey.**

9. **Fig. S9 — shark (gnathostome) trigeminal pattern.** In *Scyliorhinus torazame*, V1 is purely sensory to the rostral head (lnp-derivatives); V2 is purely sensory to the upper jaw (uj) + nasal region (lnp + fnp derivatives); V3 carries the motor fibres to mandibular-arch-derived muscles and distributes to uj-lj region. Palatoquadrate (upper jaw) + Meckel's cartilage (lower jaw) are both first-arch derivatives. **Cyclostome V2/3A and B do not map onto gnathostome V2 and V3** — the cyclostome subdivision is independent of the gnathostome jaw-based subdivision. Motor fibres in V2/3A int of cyclostomes have no gnathostome homologue.

10. **Fig. S10 — pharyngeal-patterning genes in *E. burgeri*.** *Pax1/9* in pharyngeal pouches (p1, p2, p3). *Tbx1/10A* in pharyngeal mesoderm (pm2, pm3) + mandibular mesoderm (mm). Pharyngeal compartments behave like those of gnathostomes at the gene-expression level.

11. **Fig. S11 — pharyngeal development patterns of hagfishes + lampreys.** Hagfish 3D models stages 40 → 53 show velum + tongue + p1–p9 development. Adult pharyngeal structure. Lamprey *LjTbx1/10* in situ at stages 24 + 26 visualizes muscle anlage. **First pharyngeal arch mesoderm (mm) differentiates into upper-lip muscle (php) + velum mesoderm (m.velum) in both clades.** Velum is located at the original position of the oropharyngeal membrane in both cyclostomes. The velum develops caudal to the oral cavity and rostral to the first pharyngeal pouch — mandibular arch derivative, not a separate arch. This is the critical homology.

12. **Fig. S12 — rhombomeres + cranial-nerve + pharyngeal-muscle correspondence in *E. burgeri*.** Six rhombomeres (r1–r6) recognizable in stage 45 hagfish hindbrain. V1 + V2/3 from r2; VII (acustico-facial) from r4. Each pharyngeal muscle innervated by a specific cranial nerve matching the lamprey pattern (Refs 14, 22). Table: Arch 1 tentacles + buccal → V2/3A int; Arch 1 tongue + velum → V2/3B; Arch 2 extrahyal → VII; Arches 3, 4 pharyngeal → IX + X; Arches 5+ branchial + cardiac → IX + X. **Cranial-nerve-to-arch correspondence is essentially identical to lampreys.**

## Integrated conclusion of the supplementary material

The hagfish-specific adult features (rostral nostril, oblique duct, caudally-opening nasohypophyseal duct, posterior gills + heart) are **post-phylotypic derived modifications** of a cyclostome craniofacial Bauplan shared with lampreys. Shared at the mid-pharyngula stage: single ectodermal nasohypophyseal plate with two bounding ectomesenchymal processes (ANP + PHP); ectodermal adenohypophysis (not endodermal, correcting prior literature); shared *Pitx*, *Lhx3/4*, *Six3/6*, *SoxB1*, *Nkx2.1* expression; **transient premandibular mesoderm that disappears without differentiation**; five-branch trigeminal pattern with specific arch-to-nerve correspondence; six rhombomeres; identical cranial-nerve-to-arch mapping.

The hagfish Bauplan is not "more primitive" than the lamprey one. It is a differently-modified cyclostome. Cyclostome monophyly, suggested by molecular data, is supported by this developmental-anatomical convergence of shared patterning at the phylotypic stage.

## Limitations

- **This PDF is supplementary info only** — the main text (Oisi et al. 2013 *Nature* 493:175–180) is not in this file. Teaching use should cite the full paper + figshare data, not this fragment.
- Hagfish embryonic material is notoriously scarce; some stages have data from only a single specimen. Stage 35 has no body-length data because the trunk was cut off before fixation (Supplementary Table 1 note).
- Gene-expression data are in situ hybridization only; no quantitative RNA-seq or single-cell data.
- Shark outgroup represented by a single stage-26 embryo (Fig. S5), limiting the gnathostome-side comparison.
- Stage cross-referencing to Dean 1899 (Supplementary Table 1) is provisional — Dean's figure numbers often corresponded to multiple embryos, making one-to-one stage matching uncertain.
- Authors use Nkx2.1/Pitx/Lhx3 orthology assignments (e.g. *PitxA*, *Lhx3/4A*) that depend on gene-tree interpretations of the hagfish genome, not resolved at the time.

## Relevance to comparative anatomy teaching

This paper supplements the main 2013 *Nature* article by showing, figure by figure, the developmental evidence that cyclostome-specific adult morphology is a derived overlay on a shared cyclostome Bauplan. Pair with:

- **Oisi, Fujimoto, Ota & Kuratani 2015** (Myxiniformes hypoglossal system — in library as `2015_Oisi-Fujimoto-Ota-Kuratani_Myxiniformes_Extant_hypoglossal-system.pdf`) — the authors' next paper, taking the same comparative-embryological approach to the cyclostome hypoglossal.
- **Fritzsch 1996** (Cephalochordata-Cyclostomata nervous system — in library) for the deeper-outgroup framing.
- **Janvier 1996** *Early Vertebrates* book (in library as `1996_Janvier_Vertebrata_Phanerozoic_early-vertebrate-anatomy-phylogeny-book.pdf`) for the fossil-and-comparative vertebrate-origin context.
- **Miyashita 2016** (jaw origin, mandibular confinement — in library as `2016_Miyashita_Vertebrata_Phanerozoic_jaw-origin-mandibular-confinement.pdf`) + **Miyashita 2020** (*Myxinikela* fossil — in library as `2020_Miyashita_Myxinoidea-stem_Pennsylvanian_anatomy.pdf`) for the fossil-side check on the cyclostome-monophyly developmental argument.
- **Ziermann, Miyashita & Diogo 2014** (cyclostome + chondrichthyan cephalic muscles — in library as `2014_Ziermann-Miyashita-Diogo_Cyclostomata-Chondrichthyes_Extant_cephalic-muscles.pdf`) for the adult-muscle side.
- **Brazeau & Friedman 2014** (gnathostome character hierarchy — in library) for the jawed-outgroup node.

**Pedagogic headline.** Don't teach cyclostomes as "primitive vertebrates"; teach them as "two surviving lineages that diverged after a shared cyclostome phylotypic stage." The hagfish tentacle and the lamprey upper lip share an embryological address (V2/3A branch) even though the adult structures look unrelated. Homology is established at the developmental address, not at the adult surface.

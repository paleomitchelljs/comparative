# Kępa et al. (2023) — The Komodo dragon's thoracic limb

## Citation

Kępa M, Tomańska A, Staszewska J, Tarnowska M, Klećkowska-Nawrot J,
Goździewska-Harłajczuk K, Kuźniarski A, Gębarowski T, Janeczek M. 2023. Functional
anatomy of the thoracic limb of the Komodo dragon (*Varanus komodoensis*).
*Animals* 13: 2895.

## What it covers

Forelimb anatomy by dissection, framed functionally. *Varanus komodoensis* is
gigantic, endemic and relict, and the authors treat it as a link between ancestral
and modern monitor lizards; field study is difficult, so dissection material of this
kind is scarce.

Their headline observations are the **predominance of saddle joints over ball
joints** and the extensive antigravity musculature — both consequences of carrying a
large body on a sprawling limb.

Companion to **Tomańska et al. (2025)** on the hindlimb and **(2024)** on the tail.

## Scored

2 occurrence rows, and they are **not on the Komodo dragon**: `latissimus-dorsi` on
*Eublepharis macularius* and on *Gekko gecko*, scored from Zaaf et al.'s tables with
the differences between the two geckos noted where they bear on the attachment. The
row records an origin from an aponeurosis beside the dorsal midline — no bony origin
— inserting tendinously on the proximal humerus.

**This source is cited for corroboration rather than as the observer.** Six
origin/insertion mentions in the whole paper: it is a functional account, and the
attachment statements it does make are general.

## Limitations

- **Not a row source.** See above.
- **No Komodo forelimb rows are scored from it**, which is a real gap: the animal is
  described but not in the terms this dataset records. A tabulated forelimb myology
  of *V. komodoensis* would close it.

## Relevance to comparative anatomy teaching

Useful for joint morphology and posture at large body size in a sprawling animal,
which is a comparison mammals cannot provide. Pair with **Zaaf et al. (1999)** for
gecko forelimbs at the other extreme of varanid-adjacent body size.
